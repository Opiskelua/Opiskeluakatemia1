# Yle Abitreenit: guidance for medium-syllabus Swedish (RB, YTL code BB), excluding the exam papers

Compiled 2026-11-01. Scope: everything on https://yle.fi/abitreenit/ruotsi and what it links to, followed one or two levels deep, plus the older Abitreenit Swedish articles that Google still indexes. The exam papers and HVPs themselves are **not** covered here. They are in `past-exams-grammar-catalogue.md` and `ytl-exam-format-hvp.md`.

**Tags**
- **[V]** I read it on the page (the article HTML is server-rendered, so I extracted the full text).
- **[U]** Unverified: search snippet only, or I could not render the content.
- **[I]** My own inference or recommendation, not stated by the source.

**Overall finding.** Abitreenit's Swedish section is thin. Beyond the exam papers, it holds:
- three **YTL sensor Q&As** (written answers to candidates' questions after RB S2025, K2026 and S2026), which are the most valuable source;
- one **teacher tips article** (2020);
- one **2015 sensor tips article** and one **2015 grammar review**;
- a 2024 **grammar quiz** ("Som vai vilken?");
- a 2025 **"tehtäväpaketti"** of seven grammar exercises (JS-embedded, not readable);
- a 2024 **Areena listening and viewing pack**.

Abitreenit has **no** writing phrase lists, **no** "kohteliaisuusfraasit" list and **no** graded sample essays for Swedish. Despite its title, the "kirjoitustehtäväpaketti" is a grammar pack, not a writing pack.

## 1. What graders and teachers say matters most

### 1.1 Writing is graded as a whole. There are no per-error deductions [V]
All three sensor Q&As repeat this, in nearly the same words:
- S2026: "Kirjoitelmissa arvioidaan kokonaisuutta. Huomioon otetaan muun muassa **sisällön ja tehtävänannon mukaisuus, tekstin ymmärrettävyys, rakenne, sanasto sekä kielen oikeellisuus**." (Writing is assessed as a whole: fit to the content and the prompt, comprehensibility, structure, vocabulary and correctness.) There is no fixed deduction for, say, a comma error. "Jos sisältö on hyvää, yksittäiset kielioppi- tai välimerkkivirheet eivät automaattisesti pudota pisteitä merkittävästi. Toisaalta **toistuvat tai ymmärrettävyyteen vaikuttavat virheet** vaikuttavat kokonaisarviointiin." (If the content is good, single grammar or punctuation errors do not automatically cost many points. Repeated errors, or errors that hurt comprehensibility, do.) — S1
- K2026: "Yksittäisistä kirjoitusvirheistä ei tehdä erillisiä pistevähennyksiä, vaan kokonaisuus (**ymmärrettävyys ja viestinnällisyys**) ratkaisee." (Single spelling errors carry no separate deductions; comprehensibility and communicativeness decide.) — S2
- K2026, on **en/ett errors**: "Yksittäisillä sukuvirheillä (en/ett) ei yleensä ole suurtakaan merkitystä, kunhan teksti muuten toimii." This applies even when one noun was given the wrong gender throughout the text, including possessives (*min* for an ett-word). — S2
- S2025: "Käytössä ei ole mitään kiinteitä pistevähennyksiä, jotka perustuisivat eri virhetyypeille." (There are no fixed deductions by error type.) This was said about adjective agreement with *mat* treated as an ett-word, and about spelling slips such as *jobintervju*. — S3
- S2026, on an **English loanword**: one English-origin word "ei automaattisesti ole paha virhe". What matters is that the meaning comes across and the text is in Swedish. — S1

### 1.2 Prompt fulfilment is the main risk [V]
- S2026 lottery-prize prompt: the student wrote about wanting a lamp but not about *why they need* the prize. The sensor's answer: what counts is "kuinka hyvin vastauksesi kokonaisuutena vastaa tähän" (how well the answer as a whole addresses this). It is a partial fulfilment, not an automatic zero. — S1
- S2026 "a day as Finland's president": the prompt asked about *the day*, *its tasks* and *what is best about it*. Writing about presidential values instead is related, but "ratkaisevaa on, miten hyvin kokonaisuus vastaa tehtävänantoon" (what decides is how well the whole answers the prompt). — S1
- S2025 school-food opinion piece: positive views are fine. "Keskeisintä on **vastata otsikkoon ja ohjannan kysymyksiin**." (The key is to answer the title and the prompt's questions.) — S3
- K2026 news-story prompt ("hauska" and "uskottava", funny and credible): "uskottava" means "tekstin tulee kuulostaa oikealta uutiselta … lukijan tulisi tunnistaa **uutisjournalismin tyyli, sävy ja rakenne**" (the text should sound like real news; the reader should recognise the style, tone and structure of news journalism). An absurd event can work if it is reported "asiallisella sävyllä, selkeällä rakenteella ja … selityksellä tai kontekstilla" (in a matter-of-fact tone, with a clear structure and some explanation or context). **Genre conventions are graded.** — S2
- K2026, a **missing headline** on a news story: "Teksti ei silloin vastaa täysin tehtävänantoa." (The text then does not fully meet the prompt.) The sensor meeting decides the consequence. **So when a prompt implies a headline, write one.** — S2
- K2026 company-website prompt: a candidate who wrote a job ad instead is "ainakin osittain" (at least partly) on task if the requested information is there. Text-type fit is judged. — S2
- S2025: the criteria ask candidates to **avoid excessive repetition**. A candidate asked whether using *skulle vore* twice is too repetitive; the sensor said it is judged case by case. — S3

### 1.3 Openings, closings, signatures and titles: not required unless the prompt asks [V]
- S2026: "Tehtävissä ei erikseen edellytetty tiettyä aloitusta tai lopetusta." No deduction "jos tekstisi muuten täyttää tehtävän vaatimukset" (if the text otherwise meets the requirements). On nicknames and titles: these were not asked for, so adding one causes no deduction, "kunhan … pysyy annetussa merkkimäärässä" (as long as it stays within the character limit). — S1
- K2026: "Oma otsikko on OK. Ja puuttuva 'Hej' ei varmaankaan ole ongelma." (Your own title is OK, and a missing "Hej" is probably not a problem.) A made-up name and date under a writing-competition entry: "Ei haittaa." (No harm.) — S2
- S2025: a social-media message may be signed or not; "Viestin sisällön välittyminen on tärkeämpää" (getting the content across matters more). Starting an opinion piece with "Hej" "tuskin vaikuttaa … alentavasti" (is unlikely to lower the score). Signing with your own first name does not disqualify the exam. — S3

**Implication [I]:** greetings and closings are not scored items, but a fitting one costs nothing and adds to "viestinnällisyys" and text-type fit. Note that a title **counts toward the character limit**, and going over the limit costs −3 or −6 p (see `ytl-exam-format-hvp.md` §3.1).

### 1.4 Short productive answers in Vocabulary and structures: near-error-free, and exact [V]
- S2026 task 16 (the new guided-dialogue task): "Tässä tehtävässä **täydet pisteet edellyttävät käytännössä virheetöntä kieltä**." (Full points require practically error-free language.) The example: *mus* (mouse) for *mos/potatismos* (mashed potato) is "sanastovirhe" (a vocabulary error). This is stricter than the essay grading. — S1
- S2025 translation (task 16): "Soveltamisen varaa ei tässä tehtävätyypissä paljoa ole, koska ohje on 'översätt'." (There is little room for paraphrase because the instruction says "translate".) A content-word error (*plånbok* for *kalender*) "Siitä menettää yhden pisteen" (costs one point). Leaving out a content word (*Jag ska se och kolla* with no *kalendern*) also loses credit. Whether *väldigt bra* is accepted for *erinomaista* ("excellent") "sensorikokous ottaa kantaa" (is for the sensor meeting to decide). — S3
- K2026 task 15.4, "Minä varaan liput, jos ensin päätämme…" (I'll book the tickets if we first decide…): a candidate's free rendering with *när vi först har bestämt* was "näyttää oikein toimivalta" (looks like it works). The task is graded by a human ("elävän ihmisen toimesta"). — S2. This links to the YTL grading correction for K2026 15.4 already noted in `ytl-exam-format-hvp.md` §6.

### 1.5 Reading and listening open answers in Finnish: be specific [V]
- S2026 4.2: answering only "nimikilpailu" (a naming competition) is too vague. The answer must show that a stork chick is being named. "Keskipitkässä oppimäärässä vastaukselta edellytetään jo sitä, että tekstin keskeinen sisältö ymmärretään riittävän tarkasti." (The medium syllabus already requires the core content to be understood precisely enough.) — S1
- S2026 10: a candidate added a cause the text never mentions (*sähkökatko*, power cut, when the text said a power line fell when a tram derailed). Don't add inferences the text does not state. — S1
- S2025 3.4: "heikentynyt" (weakened) does not get the point when the text says *hade gått sönder*, which means broken ("hajonneet"). **The precise Finnish equivalent is required.** — S3
- S2025 13.x: "isompi painos" (bigger edition) ≠ "suurempi fontti" (bigger font); "Emme spekuloi tekstin ulkopuolisilla tekijöillä" (we do not speculate beyond the text). — S3
- S2025 8: picture matching asks for the picture that fits *best*: "Välj den bild som bäst passar ihop med beskrivningen". Details such as *peräkärry* vs *asuntovaunu* (trailer vs caravan) decide it. — S3
- S2025 9.5: in a sentence-completion bank, the answer was *när du svarar*. The distractor *att du ska stanna genast* fit nowhere, and the candidate who put it in twice got both wrong. K2026 5: you cannot use the same option twice. — S2, S3

### 1.6 Listening audio is deliberately varied [V]
- Accents, speech impediments and background noise are intended. "Maailmassa on erilaisia puhujia ja aksentteja" (the world has all kinds of speakers and accents, S2025). Recordings test "sopeutumista erilaisiin puhetilanteisiin ja puhujien puhetapoihin" (adapting to different speaking situations and speakers' styles), and "taustaääniä" (background noise) is included on purpose (S2026). Candidates complained about "paljon riikinruotsia" (lots of Sweden Swedish) in K2026. — S1, S2, S3
- K2026 task 4 offered the options as both text and audio, because "intonaatiolla ja painotuksilla voi olla merkitystä tulkinnassa dialogeissa" (intonation and stress can matter when interpreting dialogues). That exam had 17 tasks; the number varies, but the total is always 299. — S2
- The S2026 interview quote shows the spoken register being tested: *typ*, *liksom*, *Vem orkar liksom…*, *sovmorgon*, *åka kollektivt*, *pendeltåget*. — S1

### 1.7 Teacher view: what students get wrong (Jukarainen, 2020) [V, T1]
- The most common errors are **verb tenses**, and **personal pronoun and noun inflection**.
- **Perfect tense formed wrongly**: "*Ha*-apuverbi saattaa olla oikein, mutta pääverbi onkin imperfektissä." (The *ha* auxiliary may be right, but the main verb is in the past tense, i.e. *har gick* for *har gått*.) There are many careless tense errors.
- **Noun form in context**: "juju on siinä, että osaa käyttää oikeaa substantiivin muotoa oikeassa yhteydessä. **Näitä kysytään joka kerta.**" (The trick is using the right noun form in the right context; this is asked every time.) His examples are *någon bil / den här bilen / min bil*. Students aiming for top grades should review the exceptions in definite and indefinite forms.
- **Pronouns**: "Kaikkein tavallisin virhe on, että monikon toinen persoona ***ni*** sekoitetaan muotoon ***de***." (The most common error is mixing up 2nd person plural *ni* with *de*.)
- **Situational dialogues are always tested**: "Kokeessa on aina tehtäviä, joissa pitää osata toimia tietyissä tilanteissa, kuten **hotellin vastaanotossa tai kaupassa**", often in the listening section. Practise dialogues aloud.
- **Read while noticing grammar**: spotting subordinate clauses, inverted word order and tenses in real texts works better than memorising rules.
- **Writing is nearly a third of the points**: vary structures ("suoraa ja käänteistä sanajärjestystä", straight and inverted word order) and use synonyms. **Avoid worn-out idioms** ("*Livet är ingen dans på rosor* hyppäävät silmille", such phrases jump out at the reader) and passives you are unsure of. **Write directly in Swedish**, not Finnish first and then translated.

### 1.8 Sensor view (Juurakko-Paavola, 2015) [V, T2]
- "Hyvä koevastaus vaatii paitsi ruotsin kielen osaamista, myös sitä, että osaat **välittää viestisi**." (A good answer needs not only Swedish skills but getting your message across.)
- Learn the core verbs **with their prepositions and objects**: *tacka någon för något, berätta om något för någon, besöka någon plats, börja med / sluta med något, ha ansvar för något, koncentrera sig på något*. She stresses that no definitive verb list exists.
- **Verbs that are easy to confuse**: *önska* vs *hoppas*, *bli* vs *komma*, *träffa någon* vs *träffas*.
- **English interference** in Swedish answers.
- Pick up phrases from texts rather than memorising idiom lists.
- The **sensor's proofreading checklist** (check one area at a time): **1. Pronominit, 2. Verbit, 3. Sanajärjestys, 4. Substantiivien ja adjektiivien taivutus, 5. Ajan- ja paikanilmaukset** (pronouns, verbs, word order, noun and adjective inflection, time and place expressions).

## 2. Writing (short 200–350 and long 400–600 characters)

### 2.1 Rubric-level advice found on Abitreenit [V]
- What is assessed (S1): content and prompt fit → comprehensibility → structure → vocabulary → correctness, judged together. Viestinnällisyys (communicativeness) carries the most weight (S2). This matches the HVP rubric in `ytl-exam-format-hvp.md` §3.1.
- Errors that **recur** or **impede understanding** cost points; isolated slips do not (S1, S2, S3).
- Avoid excessive repetition (S3).
- Follow the genre: news style for news, website-introduction style for a company page, letters-page style for opinion (S2, S3).
- Answer every sub-question in the prompt (the "why" in S1, "otsikko + ohjannan kysymykset", title plus the prompt's questions, in S3).
- Vary word order, use synonyms, avoid clichés and risky passives, and write directly in Swedish (T1).
- Old 2015 hub (https://yle.fi/a/20-149641): "Yleensä pärjää hyvin, kun **ei yritä liikaa**, eikä yritä kääntää omia suomenkielisiä ajatuksiaan suoraan" (you usually do well if you don't overreach and don't translate your Finnish thoughts directly). [V]
- G1 style tips: *s*-passive suits instructions and is "kohtuullisesti käytettynä" (in moderation) fine in an essay; *bli*-passive is "taitavan kirjoittajan hallinnassa erinomainen" (excellent in the hands of a skilled writer). With *man*, "ole johdonmukainen. Älä vaihda sitä kesken kaiken englanninmalliseen *du*-sanaan" (be consistent; don't switch to English-style *du* midway). [V]

## 3. Listening and reading strategy

### 3.1 Areena listening and viewing pack (L1, 2024) [V]
- The RB exam tests understanding of "erilaisten tilanteiden ja tapahtumien" (different situations and events). Practise both **Finland-Swedish and Stockholm Swedish** ("niin suomenruotsalainen kuin tukholmalainenkin sananparsi"), dialects, regional variation, and old vs young speakers.
- Method: watch with your **eyes closed or subtitles off**, then ask yourself: what was it about; what was hardest; did I notice regional differences (dialect or accent); did I notice slang or dialect words?
- Reading: exam texts are usually built **around a single topic**. Recent topics named include **sleeplessness (unettomuus), housing (asuminen) and holidays (lomailu)**. Identify the main topic early, because knowing the topic vocabulary helps.
- Turn on Swedish subtitles (*programtextning*) to practise reading while you listen.
- Vocabulary: "Yo-kirjoitusten sanastotehtävät ovat usein **aukko- tai monivalintatehtäviä**" (vocabulary tasks are often gap-fill or multiple choice). Build topic vocabularies (medicine, economy, housing) as mind maps.
- Recommended materials:
  - *Gaslight* (fast speech, context helps)
  - *Finlands historia – som du aldrig har sett den förr* (Finland-Swedish comedy)
  - *Ted & Kaj* podcast (casual talk)
  - Svenska Yle news articles: an obituary (life-stage vocabulary) and a Rauma crime report (the passive and crime-news register)
  - *Knark* (crime and drugs vocabulary)
  - *Sportliv*
  - *Livet – berättelser från Svenskfinland*
  - *Sällskapet* podcast
- The ruotsi hub also currently links *Sekoilua* (Strula, Swedish teen drama, https://areena.yle.fi/1-62643033) and *Unelmakoti* (Husdrömmar, SVT, https://areena.yle.fi/1-70768707). [V]
- T1 adds the **Sommarpratare** programmes (Vegas Sommarpratare), which are calm and easy to follow, and lots of input outside the textbook.

### 3.2 Answering technique (from the sensor Q&As; see §1.5)
- Translate the key word exactly (*gått sönder* → hajonneet).
- Include the identifying detail (whose naming competition).
- Never add causes the text does not give.
- Pick the "best" match, not just a possible one.
- An option cannot be reused.

### 3.3 Situational and politeness phrases
Abitreenit has **no** "kohteliaisuusfraasit" list [V, negative]. The only guidance is T1's "hotellin vastaanotossa tai kaupassa" (at a hotel reception or in a shop) dialogues, and practising dialogues aloud. The old adult-education course **"Javisst – palvelualan ruotsia"** (service-sector Swedish for shops, hotels, restaurants, care work and so on; https://yle.fi/a/20-146195, 10+ years old) is linked from the old Ruotsi hub. It is not exam material, but it is a possible model for situational dialogues [V for its existence; not reviewed in depth]. The actual situational-phrase items seen in exams are listed in `past-exams-grammar-catalogue.md` §6.

## 4. Quiz and exercise content on Abitreenit

### 4.1 "Som vai vilken? Både ja och!" (Q1, Sept 2024) [V]
A 17-page swipeable card story with 9 three-option MC items and a score at the end. It has three rule cards, each followed by three gap items:
1. **Correlative pairs (sanaparit)**: *både–och / såväl–som* (sekä–että), *varken–eller*, *antingen–eller*, *än–än*, *ju–desto*. Example item: "Hon är ___ hjälpsam ___ trevlig." (options *än–än / ju–desto / både–och*).
2. **Relative pronouns *som* vs *vilken/vilket/vilka***: *som* "käy melkein aina" (works almost always); *vilken/vilket/vilka* must agree with the antecedent. Example item: "Landet, ___ vann mest guldmedaljer…" (options *vilka / vilken / vilket*).
3. ***sin/sitt/sina* vs *hans/hennes/deras***: use *sin* when the owner is the subject of the same clause (Finnish *-nsa/-nsä*), and inflect it for the possessed noun.

The items are lifted from the 2015 G1 grammar page.

### 4.3 Grammar review page G1 (2015) [V]
Contents:
- Tense sequence (past vs present-tense framing, with exceptions such as *Jag har snuva i dag, därför att jag förkylde mig i går*)
- *hans* vs *sin*
- Subordinate word order with the mnemonic **"KONSUKIEPRE"** (konjunktio – subjekti – kieltosana/liikkuva määre – predikaatti: conjunction, subject, negation or movable adverb, verb), plus a list of subordinating conjunctions (*att, eftersom, då, när, innan/förrän, medan, sedan, om, fast(än)*)
- Main-clause inversion, and adverbs that never go first (*verkligen, faktiskt, förstås, väl, ju*)
- Coordinating conjunctions (*men, för, och, eller, utan*) and *kanske* allowing either order
- *s*-/*bli*-/*vara*-passives
- Present and past participles (*sovande, leende; tvättad/tvättade*)
- Correlative pairs
- *som* vs *vilken* (preposition placement *Matcherna, som jag tittade på*; *vilket* for a whole clause)

**Caution:** the page contains errors. Examples: "*Dörrarna var varit stängda*" (for *har varit*), "*såval*", and "Partisiin". Don't copy example sentences without checking them.

## 6. Implications for our course content [I]

1. **Two marking modes, taught explicitly.** In essays, communication first: one en/ett slip is cheap, while repeated or meaning-blocking errors and repetition are expensive. In guided dialogue and translation, precision first: every content word rendered, near error-free. Build separate drills and feedback rubrics for each.
2. **Prompt-checklist drill for every writing task**: underline each sub-question in the Finnish prompt (what / why / best part …) and tick each one off. Add a "genre markers" card per text type: news (headline, *I går…*, neutral tone, *enligt*), website introduction (*Välkommen till…*, *Vi erbjuder…*), opinion piece (*Jag tycker att…*, *Därför…*), social-media post, competition entry, prize-draw entry. Teach that a title counts toward the character limit.
3. **Production error drills** on what teachers and sensors name, before anything rarer:
   - *har* + supine (*har gått*, not *har gick*)
   - *ni* vs *de* vs *dem*
   - noun form in context (*någon bil / den här bilen / min bil*)
   - *sin/sitt/sina* vs *hans/hennes/deras*
   - KONSUKIEPRE (BIFF) and V2 inversion
   - verbs with their prepositions (*tacka för, berätta om … för, koncentrera sig på, ha ansvar för, börja/sluta med*)
   - confusable verbs: *önska/hoppas, bli/komma, träffa/träffas*
   - time and place expressions
4. **Teach the sensor's 5-step proofreading routine** (pronouns → verbs → word order → noun/adjective inflection → time/place) as the final step of every writing exercise, with the 200–350 and 400–600 character counter.
5. **Small units to add** that Abitreenit covers and our catalogue doesn't rank: correlative pairs (*både–och, varken–eller, antingen–eller, ju–desto, inte bara–utan*), *som* vs *vilket* (*vilket* for a whole clause), numerals and dates, and *om* + *skulle* conditionals (*Om jag vore/hade…, skulle jag…*). Use the 3-option MC format.
6. **Listening practice with variety**: Finland-Swedish and Sweden-Swedish, young speakers' slang (*typ, liksom, orka, sovmorgon, åka kollektivt*), background noise, and audio-plus-text options. For Finnish open answers, train "exact Finnish equivalent + identifying detail, no extra inference".
7. **Situational dialogue mini-units** (shop, hotel reception, café, returns, invitations) with short phrase sets, practised aloud. They feed both listening and the guided-dialogue task. Combine them with the exam-attested phrases in `past-exams-grammar-catalogue.md` §6.
8. **Style guidance in lessons**: vary word order, use synonyms, avoid clichés (*Livet är ingen dans på rosor*), use the passive only when you are sure of it, keep *man* consistent, and draft directly in Swedish.
