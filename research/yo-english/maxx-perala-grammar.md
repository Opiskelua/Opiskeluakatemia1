## 0. Read this first: caveats that matter for exercise generation

1. **His tense terminology uses *imperfekti*, not *preteriti*.** Every deck says *preesens, imperfekti, perfekti, pluskvamperfekti, futuuri, I konditionaali, II konditionaali*, plus *kesto-* forms (kestopreesens, kestoimperfekti…). He never writes "preteriti", "simple past" or "past continuous". If our students know "preteriti", map it to his "imperfekti". Other terms he uses:
   - *Y3* = yksikön 3. persoona
   - *perusmuoto* = infinitive, in four forms: the "lyhyt perusmuoto" *to see* and the "pitkä perusmuoto" *to have seen*, each in active and passive
   - *tunnus* = the *to* marker
   - *3. muoto* = past participle
   - *lauseenvastike* = participle or infinitive clause
   - *korrelaatti* = relative antecedent
   - *johtolause* = reporting clause
   - *SPOTPA* = Subjekti–Predikaatti–Objekti–Tapa–Paikka–Aika
   - *paljoussanat* = quantifiers
   - *vaillinaiset apuverbit* = modals
   - *S-genetiivi / of-genetiivi*
   - *C-sana / U-sana* = countable / uncountable
   - *muodollinen subjekti* = it/there
   - *sequence of tenses* = *aikamuotojen mukautuminen*
2. **Most of his 2025–2026 additions are AI-generated (ChatGPT) text pasted onto the site.** This covers the big articles page, "Common mistakes… for Finns", the comma page, all the YO-exam answer analyses, bad-grammar, English structures, and the concise-style pages. Several pages say so themselves ("Source: Chatty Gepetto"), and others carry the tell-tale "Here is a clear, student-friendly…" or "One classy closing line:" phrasing. **That AI content contains real errors** (listed in §22). His own older material (the 14 PowerPoint/PDF decks, the reported-speech article, the "Opiskele nämä asiat" page, the error-analysis commentary) is more reliable, but it also has slips. Do not treat the site as an infallible oracle; treat it as "what Finnish students are taught" and cross-check against Swan or Cambridge.
3. **His tense deck covers *form* only.** It never contrasts perfekti with imperfekti in use (for example, no "finished time expression → imperfekti" rule). Usage rules for the present perfect have to come from mainstream references.
4. **There is no dedicated page on prepositions, phrasal verbs or indefinite pronouns as a rule set.** He openly says the indefinite-pronoun PowerPoint was never made. These topics exist only as linked exercise files (.ppt/.doc) and lists.

## 3. Tenses: Englannin kielen aikamuodot
Source: Aikamuodot 2.0 PDF; englannin-kieliopin-perusasioita page.

**Concepts.** *Yleisaikamuoto* means the action is "ohi, valmis, toistuvaa tai yleistotuus" ("I found a watch on the street. / Water boils at 100°C. / Phil has never been abroad."). *Kestoaikamuoto* "korostaa, että tekeminen on kesken tietyllä hetkellä" ("I am learning English at the moment. / Bob has been looking for a new flat.").

**His recognition tip:** to identify a Finnish tense, turn a negative into a positive first ("Emme ostaneet venettä" → "Ostimme veneen" = imperfekti).

**Finnish meaning keys:** futuuri = *mitä tulee tekemään*; preesens = *mitä tekee*; imperfekti = *mitä teki*; perfekti = *mitä on tehnyt*; pluskvamperfekti = *mitä oli tehnyt*; I kond. = *mitä tekisi*; II kond. = *mitä olisi tehnyt*.

**Formation table** (myönteinen / kielteinen / kysyvä / kielteiskysyvä):
- I will help you / I won't help you / Will I help you? / Won't I help you?
- I help you, She helps me / I don't help you, She doesn't help me / Do I help you?, Does she help me? / Don't I help you?, Doesn't she help me?
- I helped / I didn't help / Did I help? / Didn't I help?
- I have helped, She has helped / haven't, hasn't / Have I helped? / Haven't I helped?
- I had helped / hadn't / Had I helped? / Hadn't I helped?
- I would help / wouldn't / Would I help? / Wouldn't I help?
- I would have helped / wouldn't have / Would I have helped? / Wouldn't I have helped?

**Core rules (verbatim gist):**
- "Englannin KIELTEISET ja KYSYVÄT lauseet tehdään APUVERBIN avulla." The positive preesens and imperfekti have no auxiliary, so *do/does/did* is added in negatives and questions.
- The contracted negative (won't, didn't) is normal; writing it out (will not) is "se muodollisempi tapa".
- Question word order is inverted (P + S): "Käänteisyys vaikuttaa vain ekaan predikaattiverbiin" (Has Bob been ill?). The same applies after question words: "Where did John buy his phone?" ("Suomen kielen sanajärjestystä ei voi seurailla.")
- **Imperfekti in negatives and questions:** "Enkussa did/didn't ovat jo menneen ajan apuverbejä. Pääverbi ei ole enää silloin menneessä ajassa" (*I didn't go*, *Did you see me?*). "Kieli on taloudellinen: yksi asia näkyy mieluusti yhdestä paikasta." He calls this a "VAARA" for Finns.
- **Three exceptions where preesens and imperfekti do NOT take do/does/did** ("Opettele nämä hyvin"):
  1. The main verb is *be*: "I'm not tired. / Is everybody here? / Why was Bill late for class?" Compare "Why did Bill come late to class?"
  2. The negative word is something other than *not*, in practice *never, none, no*: "I never tease my little sister. / None of the students failed to turn up." Compare "I don't often tease…"
  3. The question asks for the subject or part of it: "Who won the World Championship? / Which horse won the race?"
     - But a negative subject question does take the auxiliary: "Which students didn't do their homework?" / "Who didn't do their homework?"
     - Unless the negator is not *not*: "Which of you never skipped a lesson in senior high school?"
- Other tenses ALWAYS have their own auxiliary: "Englannin kieliopissa voi harvoin sanoa AINA, mutta edellinen dia pystyi siihen."
- Emphatic *do* (I do like you / I did go) is reserved for emphasis, "saa käyttää kerran kuukaudessa".

**Progressive (kesto-) forms:** "BE + -ing-muoto pääverbistä; olla-verbi osoittaa taivutuksellaan aikamuodon." Uses:
- kestopreesens: "Can't you see that I'm trying to sleep!"
- kestoimperfekti = background interrupted by the simple past: "Phyllis was reading a book when her boyfriend texted her…"
- kestoperfekti: "homma jatkuu vielä" ("I have been looking for a new flat for two weeks now")
- kestofutuuri: a certain near-future event ("We will shortly be arriving in Lewisham")
- kestopluskvamperfekti, also interrupted by the simple form

Full conjugation of *be*: will be / am-is-are / was-were / has-have been / had been / would be / would have been.

**Typical Finnish mistakes he names:** a past form after *did* ("did went"); forgetting do-support; Finnish word order in questions ("Why I think so?", see §6 and Appendix A).

**Flags (§22):** there are no usage rules for perfect vs past (for example *yesterday/ago* + past simple, *since/for*); non-progressive (stative) verbs are not covered; *going to*/present-for-future are not covered. The slide typo "I wouldn' have been learning" is harmless.

## 4. Sequence of tenses (aikamuotojen mukautuminen)
Source: SoT PDF, Ehtovirkkeet PDF, epäsuora-esitys page.

**The table (his central device; "voisi vaikka huoneentauluksi kehystää"):**

| Menneisyys (toiminta OHI) | Nykyaika/Tulevaisuus (toiminta KESKEN/TULEVAA) |
|---|---|
| PLUSKVAMPERFEKTI ("mitä oli tehnyt") | PERFEKTI ("mitä on tehnyt") |
| IMPERFEKTI ("ja sitten teki") | PREESENS ("mitä tekee") |
| KONDITIONAALI ("ja mitä tulisi tekemään"; II kond. = olisi tehnyt) | FUTUURI ("ja mitä tulee tekemään") |

Signature example:
- "Maxx **has** always **liked** Led Zeppelin, he **listens** to Led Zep every week and he **will listen** to them till the end of his days." ("Maxxin elämää kaikkina päivinä")
- vs "Maxx **had** always **liked** Led Zeppelin, he **listened**… and he **would listen**…" ("Maxxin muistokirjoituksesta")

**Rule:** "Yhdessä virkkeessä esiintyy yleensä vain jommankumman listan aikamuotoja." Use past tenses in an essay narrating finished events and present tenses if the action is ongoing. "I Kond. toteutui, II Kond. ei toteutunut."

**Four applications he lists:** (1) essays; (2) WISH vs HOPE; (3) conditionals; (4) reported-speech backshift.

**YO claim:** "sequence of tenses-ajattelulla voit saada 5–10 p ylioppilaskirjoituksissa. Moni täydennyskohta voi ratketa tällä perusteella." In the multiple-choice verb items, "katso koko ympäröivän virkkeen toimintaa. Kummalla puolella taulukkoa siinä liikutaan?"

**WISH/HOPE:** "WISH-verbillä toivotaan mahdottomia eli käytetään menneitä aikamuotoja. HOPE-verbillä toivotaan mahdollisia asioita, joten käytetään nykyaikamuotoja."
- "I wish I hadn't sent that e-mail. (But I did.)"
- "I wish I was in London at a blues bar. (Sadly, I'm not.)"
- "I wish you wouldn't scare me like that in future."
- "I hope I haven't offended you. / I hope you're right! / I hope you will get better soon!"

**Exception he notes elsewhere:** the *historical/dramatic present* in a past narrative "sotii periaatteessa sequence of tenses-ajattelua vastaan… lautakunta näkee aina toisinaan tilaisuutensa tulleen" (it is tested precisely because textbooks don't mention it).

**Flags:**
- (a) "Yhdessä virkkeessä vain jommankumman listan aikamuotoja" is a heuristic. Mixed-time sentences are normal: "I *have known* her since we *were* kids", "He *left* yesterday and *will be* back on Monday", "The book I *read* last year *is* still my favourite". **Do not mark mixed-tense sentences wrong on the basis of this table.**
- (b) "I wish I **was**": mainstream accepts it as informal; "I wish I **were**" is the formal/written norm (Swan). Exercises should accept both and prefer *were* in formal register.
- (c) *wish + would* is for a wished change in someone else's behaviour; it is unnatural with your own actions ("I wish I would…"). He does not say this.

## 5. Conditionals (ehtovirkkeet)
Source: Ehtovirkkeet PDF; SoT PDF.

**Three types, by "toteutumisen todennäköisyys":** *todennäköinen / mahdollinen / mahdoton* ("menneitä ei voi muuttaa"). He gives no numbers (type 1/2/3) and no zero conditional in the deck (the hub page links a video that "esittelee ZERO conditional, uutena suomalaisille").

| | Sivulause (if-) | Päälause |
|---|---|---|
| Todennäköinen | PREESENS: if I have time | FUTUURI: I will help you |
| Mahdollinen | IMPERFEKTI: if I had time | I KONDITIONAALI: I would help you |
| Mahdoton | PLUSKVAMPERFEKTI: if I had had time | II KONDITIONAALI: I would have helped you |

- **Mnemonic:** "PREESENS ja FUTUURI ne yhteen soppii, pannaan huomenna pussauskoppiin!" (the same for IMPERFEKTI + I KOND and PLKV + II KOND). Each pair sits on the same side of the SoT table.
- **Clause order:** the clauses are interchangeable, the word order stays straight, and "aloittava sivulause erotetaan pilkulla" ("If you ask nicely, I will help you.").
- **Finnish contrast:** Finnish uses the conditional in both clauses ("jos minulla olisi aikaa, auttaisin"). English does not, which is the classic error.
- **Other openers for the probable type:** *provided that, unless, in case*, and time conjunctions *when, as soon as, before, after, while, as* (with present for future: "Will you mow it as soon as you're paid?").
- **Rule of thumb:** "WOULD ja IF eivät esiinny samassa lauseessa", with exceptions:
  - if you **should** = "sattuisi": "If you should find a wallet in the street, would you keep the money?"
  - if you **would** = "viitsisi": "I could pass more easily if you would move a little."
  - would like = want: "If you would like to come, you're welcome."
  - polite "If you would take a seat, the doctor will see you in a while."
- **"WILL ja IF eivät esiinny samassa lauseessa"**, with exceptions:
  - the condition itself lies in the future: "I will study harder if it will get you off my case, mum!"
  - will = insist on: "If you will eat that much chocolate every day…"
  - won't = refuse: "If you won't come with us, there's nothing we can do"
  - polite "If you'll just fill in this form…"
- **Inversion** (in the Sanajärjestys deck): "Had I known this, I wouldn't have come at all."
- **Translation examples** (good test items):
  - "Jos ei olisi niin kylmä, menisin kävelylle" → "If it wasn't so cold, I would go for a walk."
  - "You won't succeed unless you work harder."
  - "Edison wouldn't have invented the electric bulb if he hadn't learned/learnt from his mistakes."

**Flags:**
- (a) **"in case" is NOT a conditional synonym of "if"** in standard British English. *In case* = "siltä varalta että" (precaution): "Take an umbrella in case it rains" ≠ "if it rains". His example "**I will attend in case I have time**" is non-standard; it should be "**if** I have time". Never generate items that treat *in case* = *if*. (In American English *in case* can mean "if" in some contexts, but not in this sentence.)
- (b) "If it **wasn't** so cold": informal; "If it **weren't**" is the formal norm. Accept both, and prefer *were* in formal essays (the error-analysis subagent also notes that he accepts "If I was").
- (c) Mixed conditionals ("If I had studied, I would be a doctor now") are not covered; they are standard and appear in advanced texts.
- (d) The zero conditional (If you heat water, it boils) is missing from his table; the present in both clauses is correct there.
- (e) "*If you were going to design an art form from scratch…*" (K2026 13.7) is a hypothetical with *were going to*, which his table does not cover.

## 6. Word order, inversion, it/there (sanajärjestys)
Source: Sanajärjestys PDF; pilkku page; Opiskele-nämä page.

- **Core rule:** "Suomen kielen sanajärjestys ei kelpaa malliksi englannin kieleen" (the grasshopper example: Finnish can permute freely, English cannot).
- **SPOTPA** = Subjekti–Predikaatti–Objekti–Tapa–Paikka–Aika: "Bob bought a new phone from Amazon yesterday." / "Mr Burns didn't pay enough taxes last year." / "Whales can be found in most oceans."
- **Adverbs of indefinite time** ("liikkuvat määreet": always, ever, never, generally, normally, occasionally, often, frequently, seldom, rarely, sometimes, usually, absolutely, almost, nearly, already, also, barely, hardly, scarcely, certainly, just, only):
  - before a one-word predicate: "Bob seldom comes late."
  - after one-word *be*: "Don is never late."
  - after the first auxiliary: "Billy has often been late." / "He has sometimes been called Lazybones."
- **Several time adverbials:** the least precise may start the sentence ("This year I'm going to do my homework well every night"). At the end, the more precise place/time comes first ("…at their club house in Oulu on the second Tuesday of the month").
- **Indirect object (objektiivi):** "SPO-muurin voi juuri ja juuri rikkoa": "I bought my girlfriend some roses" without a preposition; or after SPO with *to/for*: "I bought some roses for my girlfriend", "I'm going to give them to her tonight".
- **Emphatic inversion (KOROSTAVA TAPA)** after: *hardly, scarcely, little, neither, never, no sooner, not, not a…, not only (… but also), not until, nowhere, only, seldom*.
  - "Never had I seen such an exciting film."
  - "Only twice has Janet been abroad."
  - "Never does Bill laugh and seldom smile."
  - *so/neither*: "I've seen 'Titanic' three times. – So have I."
  - Conditional: "Had I known this, I wouldn't have come at all."
- **Question order:** 1 kysymyssana – 2 apuverbi – 3 subjekti – 4 pääverbi ("What should I do?", "Where does the band perform tonight?").
  - Subject questions take no do: "Who knows grammar well?", "Which horse won the race?"
  - Other auxiliaries work normally: "Which students have done their homework?"
  - When *who/which* is the object, all tenses use an auxiliary: "Who(m) did you see at the party?", "Which phone do you want to buy?", "Who have you been listening to?"
- **Passive sentences:** Finnish starts with the place adverbial ("Suomessa tehdään hyviä autoja"), English keeps "määreet loppuun": "Good cars are made in Finland."
- **Subjects are obligatory:** "Englannissa on aina subjekti" (Sataa lunta → It is snowing).
  - **IT** for time, distance, opinion, weather and emphasis: "It's 8.30 pm. / It's a five-minute walk. / It's fun to go swimming. / It was two years ago that I met her."
  - **THERE** for "jossakin on jotakin": "There is a fly in my soup! / There are people who pick their noses… / There was a strong wind."
  - Finnish begins with the place ("Pihalla oli jänis"); English puts the place at the end and introduces the new thing as indefinite: "There was a rabbit in the yard." vs "The rabbit was in the yard."
- **Main and subordinate clauses have straight order**, "vaikka sivulause aloittaisi virkkeen" ("Because you are my friend, I will buy you an ice-cream"), unlike Swedish (*omvänd ordföljd*).
- **Indirect questions** take straight order (see §8). The error analyses show "how would I solve it" type errors in 4 of 5 years (Appendix A).
- **YO:** it/there items appear in the exam ("It was / It has / There was / There has"; "it was / there were / that was / they were").

**Flags:**
- (a) Slide 74 says the clauses are "SUORIA **(P + S)**". That is a typo for S + P; don't copy it.
- (b) "Never does Bill laugh and seldom smile" is odd English; avoid it as a model.
- (c) The listed placements for "also, only, just" are simplified; *only* moves to focus.
- (d) *Little* triggers inversion only in "Little did I know…" type uses.

## 7. Passive (passiivi)
Source: Passiivi PDF; Vaillinaiset apuverbit PDF; YO analyses.

- **When to use it:** "Käytä passiivia, kun et tiedä tekijää tai halua korostaa sitä." It is "ei niin tavallinen englannissa kuin suomessa", "esiintyy lähinnä kirjoitetussa kielessä", and is good for style in an essay ("ei ainoana työkaluna").
- **Formation:** *be* + 3. muoto; *be* carries the tense and the number.
  - "Breakfast is served from 7.30 till 9.30 a.m."
  - "The bank robbers were caught very quickly."
  - "Xylitol was invented in Finland."
  - "This house was built in 1957. / Those houses were built…"
- **Full paradigm:**
  - is/are cleaned (PR)
  - was/were cleaned (IMP)
  - has/have been cleaned (PERF)
  - had been cleaned (PL KV)
  - will be cleaned (FUT)
  - would be cleaned (I KOND)
  - would have been cleaned (II KOND)
  - must be cleaned (apuverbit)
  - modal past: must/can/could/may/might/will/shall/would/ought to **have been seen** ("PS Näitä kysytään kirjoituksissa kun helpommat muodot eivät erottele tarpeeksi.")
- **Progressive passive** only in kestopreesens and kestoimperfekti, with "BEING lisätään be-verbin jälkeen": "The president is being interviewed on TV! / The walls were being painted when I came in."
- **get-passive** in everyday language: "I got lost in a forest. / Donna got married. / My aunt got struck by lightning, but luckily she didn't get killed."
- **Agent with *by***: "The Shining was written by Stephen King" (Finnish uses the active here: Stephen King kirjoitti Hohdon).
- **Finnish passive → English active with an indefinite subject** (*you, we, they, people, one*):
  - you = people in general and instructions ("How do you open this box?")
  - we = you belong to the group ("We eat a lot of rye bread in Finland")
  - they = you don't belong to the group, or the authorities ("They drive on the left in Japan", "They raised the price of petrol again")
  - people (plural)
  - one ("harvinainen ja muodollinen", takes the 3rd-person -s: "One never knows…")
- **Finnish impersonal without an object:** "Kadulla tapeltiin" → "There was fighting in the street"; "Oveen koputettiin" → "There was a knock on the door"; "Suomessa pyöräillään paljon" → "We cycle a lot in Finland."
- **Finnish active forms that are passive in English:** be amazed, born, destroyed, disappointed, drowned, hurt, injured, killed, located, mistaken, shocked, situated, surprised. "Two people were killed in the accident." "I was shocked when he told me about it." "The teacher was surprised."
- **be born:** Göran syntyi / on syntynyt / oli syntynyt → "Göran was born in Sweden": "Käytä be born-rakennetta imperfektissä."
- **Mediopassive (active in English, passive in Finnish):** "His book is selling well. / Wool doesn't wash well."
- **Formula:** Kohde alkuun – olla-verbi – pääverbi 3. muodossa – määreet loppuun – (agentti): "Good cars are made in Finland by skilled workers."
- **YO:** "Aktiivimuotoinen vai passiivimuotoinen predikaatti?… noin kerran per koe ratkaisun avain. Katso lauseen aloittajaa: jos aloittaja tekee jotain → aktiivi; jos aloittaja on kohde → passiivi." K2026 had *were reflected, is dominated, was struck*; S2025 had *has been made, have been overdone, could have been left out*.

**Flags:**
- (a) "Englannin kielen passiivit alkavat **aina** tekemisen kohteella" is false as an absolute. Prepositional passives ("The bed has been slept in") and *it*-passives with reporting verbs ("It is said that…", "He is said to be…") exist and are frequent in YO texts. He does not cover *be said/thought/believed to*, *have something done*, or passives of two-object verbs ("I was given a book").
- (b) Being born: "Käytä be born imperfektissä" is right for living people, but "has been born" is possible for recent births ("A baby has been born").

## 8. Reported speech (epäsuora esitys / raportoitu puhe)
Source: Epäsuora PDF; the "Epäsuora esitys SELKEÄSTI" article (Maxx's own); SoT PDF.

- **Direct speech markers:** johtolause, colon, quotation marks, verbatim quote ("Mum says: "I will make pancakes!""). In indirect speech the johtolause stays, the colon and quotes go, and the pronouns change perspective.
- **Present johtolause** ("nykyraportointi", "puhelinraportointi"): all tenses are kept. "Mum says she will make pancakes / is tired / went to the gym / has seen a flying saucer…"
- **Past johtolause:** "menneen ajan raportointi käyttää vain menneen ajan aikamuotoja."
  - Pluskvamperfekti, imperfekti and the konditionaalit do not change; imperfekti "voi halutessasi muuttua pluskvamperfektiksi" (both correct; plkv sometimes more precise).
  - **Perfekti → pluskvamperfekti; preesens → imperfekti; futuuri → I konditionaali.**
  - can → could; may → might (may in the sense of permission is reported as could); must → had to if the obligation has been fulfilled, otherwise *must* stays.
  - Unchanged: would, should, could, might, ought to, needn't, had better. *shall* (future) → *would*.
  - A perfekti johtolause ("has said") counts as present, so there is no backshift.
- **Optional backshift (deck slides 39–41 and the article):** a general truth ("The teacher told us (that) water boils at 100 °C"); an unchanged situation ("Ben said he enjoys reading poems"). "Näissäkin tapauksissa aikamuodon voi muuttaa menneeksi, jos haluaa."
  - Note that the SoT deck states the stricter "vain menneitä aikamuotoja"; the reported-speech deck and the article give these exceptions. Use the latter.
- **Pronouns:** decided case by case. Brett to Liz: "I saw you at the airport" →
  - "I told Liz I had seen her…" (Brett reports)
  - "Brett told me he had seen me…" (Liz reports)
  - "Brett told Liz he had seen her…" (an outsider reports)
- **"Merkkisanat" (time and place words):**
  - this → that; these → those; here → there
  - now → then; today → that day; tonight → that night
  - yesterday → the day before / the previous day; tomorrow → the following day / the next day
  - next week → the following week; last week → the week before; a week ago → a week earlier
  - *often, sometimes* and exact places do not change.
  - Example: "I will meet you here tomorrow." → "Robert told me he would meet me there the following day."
- **Yes/no questions:** if/whether + straight word order (S+P), no question mark (unless the johtolause is itself a question), and the **positive do/does/did disappears ("DO-miinustus")**: "Do you love me?" → "Jane asked her boyfriend if he loved her."
  - **The negative do is kept:** "Don't you love me anymore?" → "…if he didn't love her anymore"; "Daria asked me if I didn't know the way."
- **Wh-questions:** question word + straight order: "What did you see in that twerp?" → "My friends kept asking me what I saw / had seen in that twerp." "Joe was wondering where he had seen that actress before." With a present reporting verb: "Tell me what you are doing."
- **Commands and requests:** to-infinitive: "The customs officer asked me to show him my passport." Negatives: not/never + to-infinitive: "Everybody told Homer… not to push that button there."
  - The imperative "ei ole aikamuoto", so there is no backshift. A typical student error is "*…to showed him*".
  - *please* is absorbed into *ask*. Negation can be lexicalised: "I told you not to leave" → "I forbade you to leave."
- He stresses that "I want to know… / Tell me… / I don't know…" are almost always followed by a reported question with straight order.
- **YO:** "Tätäkin asiaa testataan ylppäreissä tavalla tai toisella."

**Flags:**
- (a) "Kun johtolause on menneessä aikamuodossa, vain menneitä aikamuotoja voi käyttää" (the SoT deck) is too strict; see the exceptions above. Swan also allows no backshift whenever the reported content is still true or relevant ("She said she's coming tomorrow").
- (b) The *must → had to* rule is stated in a slightly idiosyncratic way. Mainstream: *must* may stay or change to *had to* for obligation; epistemic *must* stays.

## 9. Modal verbs (vaillinaiset apuverbit)
Source: Vaillinaiset apuverbit PDF; Infinitiivi PDF.

- **Inventory:** can/could, may/might, must, will/would, shall/should, ought to (plus dare, need in the infinitive deck).
- **Properties:** no infinitive form, no -ing, no Y3 -s, no do in questions or negatives, not all tenses possible.
- **CAN = osaaminen/kykeneminen** (present/future): "Harold can fix cars. / Can't you speak Swedish?" Permission: "Can/Could I borrow your skis?"
- **COULD:** past ability ("I could read when I went to school. / I just couldn't tell her the bad news") and polite questions ("Could you open the door for me?").
  - Other tenses use **be able to**: "Have you been able to sleep lately? / It's great to be able to sing well."
  - Also *know how to, be unable to, manage to, it's (im)possible for … to*: "Bob knew how to drive a tractor. / I was unable to lift the box. / Julian managed to pay back his loan."
- **MAY:** permission or possibility ("May we go now? / It may rain tomorrow. / You may not smoke here!"). **MIGHT:** "arvelu" ("You might be right"). Other tenses use **be allowed to**: "As a child I wasn't allowed to watch TV late. / Women have never been allowed to vote…"
- **MUST:** obligation (present): "You must return this book tomorrow." **mustn't = kielto**: "You mustn't tell this to anybody!" **don't have to = ei tarvitse**: "You don't have to wait for me." Other tenses use **have to**: "I had to tell the truth. / Robert has always had to work hard."
  - Informal: have got to, gotta ("Gotta ei käy kirjoitettuun kieleen").
  - **must vs have to:** must = "puhujan itsensä asettama pakko" ("I must stop smoking! … I smell like an ashtray"); have to = "pakko tulee muualta" ("…I don't have any money!").
- **Epistemic scale ("arvelua"):**
  - must be right (olen varmasti)
  - can be right (voin olla) ⚠
  - could be right (voisin olla)
  - may be right (saatan olla)
  - might be right (saattaisin olla)
  - would be right (olisin)
  - will be right
  - should / ought to be right (minun pitäisi olla)
- **Modal + infinitive:** present reference = "lyhyt perusmuoto" (must see, can see…); past reference = "pitkä perusmuoto" (must have seen, could have seen, should have seen…). Passive: must be seen / must have been seen.
  - He notes that the should buy / should have bought difference is often unknown and so tested. "PS Näitä kysytään kirjoituksissa kun helpommat muodot eivät erottele tarpeeksi."
- **YO:** S2025 item "could well have been left out" (modal + have been). Option types: "may / must / ought / shall", "can't / couldn't / weren't able / won't be able".
- **will/would for habit** (Opiskele-nämä page): present habit "The girl next door **will sing** karaoke every night"; past habit "My granny **would sit** in her rocking chair for hours knitting socks".

**Flags:**
- (a) **"I can be right" = "Voin olla oikeassa" is unidiomatic.** Mainstream (Swan, Cambridge): positive epistemic *can* is not used for a specific present possibility. Use *may/might/could be right*. *Can* is fine for general possibility ("It can be cold in June") and in the negative ("That can't be right").
- (b) **"can have seen"** in his list exists essentially only in negatives and questions ("He can't have seen it", "Can he have forgotten?"). Positive "*He can have seen it*" is wrong; use *could/may/might have seen*.
- (c) Slide 78 reads "MUST ilmaisee pakkoa (prees.), have to = ei tarvitse". He means **don't have to** = ei tarvitse; *have to* alone = must. Make sure exercises don't copy the garbled version.
- (d) The must/have to internal/external split is a BrE tendency, not a hard rule; in AmE *have to* dominates for both.
- (e) "shall have seen / will have seen" are listed as modal past forms; *will have seen* is really the future perfect or a deduction ("You will have seen the news").
- (f) *needn't have done* vs *didn't need to* is not covered.

## 10. Infinitive, -ing form or that-clause (Infinitiivi, -ing-muoto vai that-lause)
Source: Infinitiivi PDF; Lauseenvastikkeet PDF; Opiskele-nämä page.

**When a verb is not the predicate, there are four cases:** perusmuoto with *to*, perusmuoto without *to*, a that-clause, or the -ing form. ("I want to be free! / I can swim well. / I believe (that) I can finish this job. / I'm interested in meeting new people.")

**Four infinitives:** to see / to have seen / to be seen / to have been seen. Negatives add *not* or *never* before *to*: "not to have been seen".
- "You should have seen my room yesterday: it was a mess!"
- "My room should have been cleaned a long time ago."

**to-infinitive after:**
- noun: "a good reason to go"
- pronoun: "somebody to love"
- adjective: "proud to be a Finn"
- verb: "I want you to go"
- question word: "I'm not sure what to do or where to go", replacing a reported question; "why not start" has no *to*
- **purpose:** "I went to the movies to see…"; *in order to / so as to*: "I tiptoed to my room so as not to wake everybody up"
- after a **superlative, ordinal, first/next/last/only** as a lauseenvastike: "The best thing to do now is to stay calm. / Who was the first man to land on the moon?"
- as subject: "To tell the truth is not always easy", though -ing or "It is…" is more usual: "Telling the truth… / It is not always easy to tell the truth."
- Verbs that take **object + to-infinitive**: *advise, allow, ask, order, permit, tell, want, would like* ("I want you to remember this! / Mother allowed me to go").

**Bare infinitive:**
- after modals: *can, could, may, might, must, will, would, shall, should, dare, need*
- after **active perception verbs**: *feel, hear, see, notice, observe, watch* ("I noticed you leave the party early")
- after **make, help, let** in the active: "Joe made me skip the maths lesson. / Daphne helped me (to) find an apartment. / They let young people come"
- after *had better, rather than, do nothing but*: "You had better start right now! / I'd like to go outside rather than stay indoors. / I did nothing but sleep"

**Passive → to returns:** "I saw you leave / You were seen to leave"; "Bob made me tell a lie / I was made to tell a lie"; "I was helped to find an apartment". *let* has no passive, so use *be allowed to*: "Young people were allowed to come."

**Perception verb + -ing** = action in progress: "You were seen talking to the teacher." "I saw him drive away" (the whole act) vs "I saw him driving away" (in progress); "ero on olemassa mutta se ei ole iso".

**that-clause** after verbs of belief, supposition and knowledge (active): *believe, suppose, know, think* ("I believe (that) I can answer…"; "Betty knew (that) I would look her up"). Some verbs take either: "He claims to know / claims (that) he knows." Adjectives too: "I'm happy to do it / I'm happy (that) you are here."

**Four -ing forms:** waking / having woken / being woken / having been woken. "I'm used to waking up early. / Having woken up I usually have some coffee. / Nobody likes being woken… / Having been woken I'm often rather grumpy."
- as a noun: "Singing is my passion"; as an adjective: "your smiling face"
- **"TÄRKEÄÄ: Verbistä käytetään aina ing-muotoa preposition jälkeen!"**: "interested in collecting stamps / no excuse for being late / objected to being sent home / thinking of quitting"

**Verbs + -ing:** *admit, avoid, can't help, can't stand, deny, dislike, enjoy, escape, fancy, finish, keep, mind, miss, practise, quit, risk* ("Jane denied having stolen anything. / I wouldn't mind being told…").

**Phrases + -ing:** *be busy, be accustomed to, be used to, get used to, be worth, feel like, it's no good, it's no use, there's nothing like* ("Mum was busy making pancakes. / I feel like dancing!").

**Either form:** *begin, start, continue; love, hate, like, prefer* ("I started to do / doing"). **Meaning changes:** *forget/remember, go on, regret, stop, try*: "I didn't remember to phone / Don't you remember phoning him? / We stopped to have lunch / We stopped having lunch."

**YO:** "Tätä sovellettua verbioppia löytyy varmasti joka kerta ylppäreistä… Lautakunta saa hyvin tällä asialla haluamaansa hajontaa", about one item per exam. Option types: "finding / founding / to find / has founded", "take / to take / taken / having taken". S2024 items: *15.15 discussing, 15.16 to speak*; K2026 had "by threatening".

**Flags:**
- (a) "You were noticed to leave the party early" is very marginal. In practice the passive + to-infinitive pattern is natural with *see, hear, make* ("was seen to leave", "was heard to say"), not with *notice/observe/watch*. Don't generate "was noticed/watched to…".
- (b) *dare, need* without *to* only in modal use (negatives/questions: "You needn't come", "How dare you"); as main verbs they take *to* ("You need to come").
- (c) The "My room should be seen now" example is odd but grammatical.
- (d) The lists are not exhaustive. Common YO patterns missing: *suggest/recommend* + -ing or that (should); *look forward to / object to / be committed to* + -ing (the "to" preposition trap); *prevent sb from doing*; *afford, manage, refuse, decide, hope, promise* + to.

## 11. Participle and infinitive clauses (lauseenvastikkeet)
Source: Lauseenvastikkeet PDF; abikurssi "Grammar Corner" (Appendix B §); pilkku page.

- **Purpose:** they condense; they are "voimamuotoja"; "tärkeintä tunnistaa ne tekstistä" (they appear "päntiönään" in YO texts and are asked in the gap-fill); in the essay "rakennepisteitä ropisee", but "ei kenenkään AINUT tehokeino".
- **Main rule:** shorten a subordinate clause that has **the same subject as the main clause**. An active clause becomes -ing; a passive clause becomes the 3rd form.
  - "The people who live here are wealthy" → "The people living here are wealthy."
  - "Many cars that are bought in Finland…" → "Many cars bought in Finland…"
  - ("Cars (that) I like…": object relative omission, a different subject.)
  - "Is the man who is wearing a red shirt…" → "the man in a red shirt" (the verb is implied)
- **Infinitive** after an ordinal, superlative or next/last/only: "the best tool to use", "Who's next to be served?"
- **Time:** simultaneous action uses -ing ("Tom did his homework listening to music"); an earlier action uses *having + 3rd form* or *after + -ing* ("Having done his homework, he watched TV. / After doing…").
  - Passive: "Asked to give a speech, I was glad" / "Being asked…" (to stress simultaneity) / "Having been asked…" (to stress sequence).
  - After/Before/Since/On + -ing: "Before leaving for school… / On noticing his mistake, Jack corrected it."
- **Reason, concession, condition:**
  - "Being 17, Mo can't go to bars."
  - "Although invited to the party, Jack didn't go."
  - "Unless mailed today, this job application will be too late."
- **Indirect question:** "Janet didn't know what to do next. / I don't know whether to laugh or cry!"
- **Coordinate clause (and):** "Jane worked very hard earning a lot of money."
- **Purpose:** to / in order to / so as to.
- **Different subject: possible if the subject is kept** (absolute construction): "The rain being so hard, we couldn't go out."
- **be-verb dropped:** "Alone, I started to think." / "Being alone, …"
- **Fixed phrases:** *generally speaking, strictly speaking, considering, judging by, supposing that, compared with/to* ("Compared with Italians, Finns are much better at playing ice-hockey").

**Flags (important):**
- (a) **Dangling participles.** His deck states the same-subject rule correctly, but the 2026 comma page (and its model essays) contains classic dangling modifiers that Swan and Cambridge treat as errors:
  - "Supported by friends, difficult situations become easier to face."
  - "Faced with high expectations both at school and at home, their mental health may suffer."
  - "Exposed to endless information, it is difficult to know what to trust."
  - "Looking at the issue from a wider perspective, simple solutions rarely exist."
  - "To succeed in modern society, strong social skills are essential." (dangling infinitive; tolerated by some, criticised by most)

  **Our exercises must enforce the same-subject rule and must not copy these sentences as models.**
- (b) "Being asked to give a speech, I was glad" is awkward; *Asked to…* or *When asked…* is natural.
- (c) "Jane worked very hard earning…" needs a comma, and the meaning shifts.
- (d) Reduced relatives with -ing cannot express a single completed past event ("the man who won the race" ≠ "*the man winning the race*").
- (e) "The rain being so hard": *heavy rain* is the collocation, and the absolute construction is literary.

## 12. Relative pronouns (relatiivipronominit)
Source: Relatiivipronominit PDF; hub page; abikurssi Grammar Corner; YO pages.

- **Korrelaatti:** a word or a whole clause ("He's a jolly good fellow, which nobody can deny").
- **Defining (välttämätön) clauses take no commas; non-defining (irrallinen) clauses take commas:** "My cousin who is an engineer works for Nokia (I've got many cousins)" vs "My cousin Pertti, who lives in a flat, likes his job." (The abikurssi test: can you insert "muuten"?)
- **WHO** for people. After a preposition **WHOM**: "one of whom is a doctor"; also *some/most/many of whom*.
- **WHOSE** for both people and things: "There's the man whose house burned down. / That building whose walls are red…"
- **WHICH** for things: "Music which I like…"
  - with a preposition: "a question to which there is no answer"; or a stranded preposition: "the DVD which I told you about"
  - which = the whole preceding clause, always with a comma: "Everybody had done their homework, which was surprising."
- **THAT** for people and things, but **never in a non-defining clause** (and never after a comma or a preposition).
- **"THAT on ainut mahdollinen":** after a head word *all, much, little, few*; after *-thing* pronouns; after a superlative; after an ordinal; after *first, last, only*.
  - "This is all that I know. / I lost everything that I had. / the best camera that money can buy / the first man that landed on the moon."
- **WHAT** = "se, mikä"; it does not refer outside itself: "What you say is true."
  - "kaikki mitä" = *all that*: "All that you say can and will be used against you."
  - **Classic error:** "*The present what I got from grandmother*" → *which/that*. Compare the reported question "I don't know what you are talking about."
- **Omission:** "Jos joka/mikä-lauseeseen jää täydellinen subjekti ja täydellinen predikaatti, saa pronominin jättää pois": "Tell me everything (that) you can remember" but "Tell me everything that happened." "There's the man about whom I was talking" = "There's the man I was talking about."
- **where/when/why** = in which / on which / for which: "the place where Olof Palme was murdered / the day when I start a new life / the reasons why you did this."
- **-ever words:** whatever, wherever, whenever, whichever, whoever ("Whoever finds my wallet…").
- **YO (Maxx):** "Lautakunta kuittaa kokelailta vinon pinon pisteitä lähes jokavuotisella what-pronominin kysymisellä"; "that-pronominin käyttörajoitteet kannattaa erityisesti tuntea"; "lautakunta kysyykin useammin that-pronominin käytön rajoituksia". Option sets: "that / what / when / which", "that / what / which / –", "which / what / who / that".
  - Real items: K2026 *in which*, *that* (an art form that changes); S2025 *which* (upon which); S2024 *15.8 which*, *15.9 which*.

**Flags:**
- (a) **"THAT is the only possibility" after superlatives, ordinals, only and all is too strong.** *That* is preferred after *all/everything/nothing/-thing*, but **who** is normal for people after a superlative, ordinal or *only* ("the first man **who** walked on the moon", "the only person **who** understood"), and his own Lauseenvastikkeet deck uses "Who was the first man **who** landed on the moon?". Exercises must accept *who* in these cases.
- (b) "After something/everything never use which" (K2025 commentary, Appendix A) is a preference; *which* occurs, though *that* is far more common.
- (c) "My cousin that is an engineer" is grammatical but *who* is preferred for people in formal writing.

## 13. Adjectives and adverbs, comparison (adjektiivit ja adverbit)
Source: Adjektiivit ja adverbit 2.0 PDF; Opiskele-nämä page; Paljoussanat PDF.

- **Definitions:** adjektiivi answers MILLAINEN?; adverbi (määre: SPOTPA's T, P, A) answers MILLÄ TAVALLA? / MITEN? Place and time adverbials go at the end.
- **Adjectives are not inflected** (unlike Swedish snabb/snabbt/snabba).
- **Comparison:** short adjectives take -er / the -est (quick, quicker, the quickest); long ones take more / the most (expensive). Irregular:
  - bad/ill – worse – the worst
  - far – farther/further – the farthest/furthest
  - old – older/elder – the oldest/eldest
  - good – better – the best
  - late – later, latter – the latest, the last
  - little/small – smaller – the smallest
- **Comparison structures:** "as good as"; negative "not so good as" (also possible); "better **than**"; "smaller and smaller / more and more expensive"; "**The more** I use my camera, **the better** photos I take" ("Mitä…sitä on enkussa the…the…, ei what…that").
- **Superlative:** "Muista superlatiivin määräinen artikkeli": "the best camera there is", "the most delicious hamburger I've ever had."
  - BUT "**a** most delicious hamburger" = "mitä herkullisin"
  - "That burger really was most delicious" (no article when predicative in this sense)
  - "at **its/her** best/most expensive" = "parhaimmillaan / kalleimmillaan": "Oil was at its most expensive in the early seventies."
- **Position:** attributive "a happy student" or predicative "the student is happy".
  - **Predicative only:** *afraid, alike, alive, asleep, aware, content, glad, ill, well*.
  - **Attributive counterparts:** *scared/frightened, similar, live/living, sleeping, conscious, contented/satisfied, happy, sick, healthy*.
- **the + adjective = a group of people:** "The young often party… / The Good, The Bad and The Ugly." For a subset use a support word: "a young person, many young people". (YO K2026 13.14: "the ostentation of **the rich**"; "a rich" and bare "rich" were the wrong options.)
- **the + adjective = an abstract concept:** "I can't do the impossible! / Are you afraid of the dark?" ("Näitä esiintyy parhaassa enkussa.")
- **Compound age/measure adjective:** "a student who is seventeen years old" → "**a seventeen-year-old student**": "Huomaa substantiivin yksikkö ja yhdysmerkit. HUOM: Ylppäreissä kysytään usein tätä." (K2024 item "31-year-old"; K2026 "aged 12".)
- **Participles as adjectives:** -ing = action in progress ("a laughing lunatic"), 3rd form = action finished ("the newly painted house").
- **Adverb formation:**
  - adjective + -ly (passionately, surely, beautifully, slowly)
  - -ic → -ically (romantically, scientifically), **except publicly**
  - adjectives ending in -ly (silly, lively, friendly, surly) → "in a silly way / in a lively manner", or a synonym (foolishly, kindly)
  - same form as the adjective: *early, fast, hard, late, long, right, wrong, daily* ("We arrived early. / Students worked hard. / Do it right. / I read English daily")
  - good → **well** ("He sings well"); "Silti porukat sanovat huolimattomasti: He sings really good" (an error)
- **Two adverb forms with different meanings:**
  - hard / hardly ("Joe hardly worked at all")
  - late / lately
  - deep / deeply ("Dig deeper / I'm deeply grateful")
  - high / highly
  - free / freely
  - near / nearly
- **Adverb comparison:** -ly adverbs take more/most (more quickly, most quickly: "Jotkut sanovat the most quickly. Minä en!"); flat adverbs take -er/-est (fast, faster, fastest); irregular: badly–worse–worst, far–further/farther, little–less–least, much–more–most, well–better–best.
- **Intensifiers:**
  - adjective/adverb base form: quite, rather, very, a bit, a little, fairly, pretty, awfully, terribly, exceptionally, extremely, really, surprisingly
  - comparatives: a lot, far, much, a little, a bit, slightly ("something a bit cheaper", "much more efficiently")
  - superlatives: of all, by far, very ("by far the fastest", "the fastest typist of all"; "Sharon types fastest of all")
- **Test:** "I bought a safe car (minkälaisen?) / I arrived safely (miten?)."
- **YO:** "life / alive / lively / living" (asked in two exams), "late / later / latter / latest", word-family items (substantial → substantially, common → commonly, S2025). Intensifier set "all / ever / really / by far" (asked in two exams).

**Flags:**
- (a) **"I think high of you" (slide 88) is wrong.** It should be "I think **highly** of you" (*high* is for literal height: "fly high"). The slide intends the high/highly pair but has the error in the example.
- (b) "I am a lot better photographer" is colloquial; standard is "a much/far better photographer" (*a lot* before a comparative + noun is informal).
- (c) "the most quickly" is acceptable in mainstream English (*the* is optional with adverb superlatives), so don't mark "ran the fastest" wrong. The AI bad-grammar page muddles this further (Appendix B §21).
- (d) Linking verbs + adjective (look/seem/feel/sound/smell *good*, not *well*/-ly) are not covered and are a common item; add them from Swan.
- (e) "late – latter" is a different meaning (the former/the latter), not simply a comparative.

## 14. Articles (artikkelit)
Source: Artikkelit PDF (Maxx's own); the 2026 "Iso artikkelikäsittely" page (AI); abikurssi Grammar Corner; Opiskele-nämä page.

**Philosophy:**
- "Suomalaiset käyttävät artikkeleita enkussa LIIAN VÄHÄN." "HARVALLA on LIIKAA artikkeleita, MONILLA LIIAN VÄHÄN."
- **Rule of thumb:** "Harkitse (C-sanalle) artikkelia, jollet ole varma, ettei sitä tule." "'Ei artikkelia' on myös artikkelivalinta."
- Check articles in a separate proofreading pass.
- **Swedish mapping:** en pojke = a boy / pojken = the boy / pojkar = boys / pojkarna = the boys.

**Position:**
- Before all modifiers ("a funny old man"), so the article can be far from the noun, which is the main danger in long noun phrases.
- **a/an comes AFTER** *half, such, quite, what* (exclamations), *rather*: "half an apple / such an exciting film / quite a good singer (also: a quite good singer) / What a lovely day! / rather a good singer (also a rather good singer)".
- **as…as, too, how, however + adjective + a + noun:** "as cheap a car as I can find / too difficult a problem for me / However good a swimmer you are…" ("paperinmakuisia"; "The problem was too difficult for me to solve" is more everyday).
- **the comes after** *all, half, both, twice, double*: "all the luck / not half the man / both (the) houses / twice the sum."

**a/an:** by sound: an heir, an MP, a honeymoon, a university (AI page: a European, a one-way street, an FBI agent, a UFO, an NGO).

**Indefinite article uses:**
- new thing ("There's a fly in my soup")
- "one" (a or one, not both)
- "eräs" ("a cousin of mine / one of my cousins")
- "sellainen" defined afterwards ("It's a book that you can't put down")
- What/such ("What a mess!")
- **profession, nationality, religion, party**: "I'm a teacher, a Finn, a Lutheran"
- rate expressions without a preposition: "twice a month, two euros a kilo, 50 km an hour"
- set phrases: have a…, make a…, take a…, go for a…, be in a… ("in a hurry")

**Definite article uses:**
- mentioned before
- clear from the situation ("Close the door")
- unique ("The Earth revolves around the sun")
- **but space and nature take no article**
- followed by an of-phrase ("the name of the game"), a prepositional phrase ("The Man with the Golden Gun") or a relative clause ("The spy who loved me")
- superlatives
- ordinals
- **sports results take no article** ("The best Finn finished eighth")
- restrictive words: *the following, the former/the latter, the last, the next, the only, the opposite, the present, the previous, the right, the wrong, the same, the usual*
- **last/next without the = viime/ensi** ("last summer, next year") vs "the last bus home, the next thing to do"
- compass areas: "in the north of Finland", **but no article via an adjective**: "in northern/central/southern Finland" (YO: "north / north of / northern / northerly"; "North-Finland" doesn't exist)
- "the more…, the more…"
- the + adjective (group or abstract)
- eras and decades ("the Space Age, the early 21st century, the noughties, the nineties")

**Zero article:**
- months, weekdays, holidays (in August, on Monday, at Christmas; but "the Christmas of 2001")
- times of day: in the morning/afternoon/evening, but **at night, at midnight, at noon, at midday**
- after a genitive (Robert's bike), possessive, *every/some/any/no*, *this/that/these/those*
- general plurals ("I like exciting films" vs "The films in this movie theatre…")
- U-nouns in general meaning (love, milk, water, beauty, steel, *but* "the love that we share")
- **institution use**: bed, hospital, jail, prison, church, town, college, school, sea, university, work, home ("go to school / in hospital / at work / go to town"); the building takes *the* ("The school was old")
- **transport and meals**: by air/land/sea/rail/road/bike/boat/bus/car/plane/taxi/train, on/by foot; but "**in** my car", "ride a bike / drive a car / take a train"; have breakfast/lunch/dinner but "a full English breakfast / a delicious lunch"
- "kind/sort/type of + no article" ("What kind of film…"); plural "These kinds of films"
- set phrases: hand in hand, face to face, from door to door, from top to bottom, come to light, keep in mind

**Proper names:**
- singular geographical names take no article (Europe, Finland, London, Mount Everest, Lake Saimaa, central Finland)
- *empire, kingdom, republic, state, union, region, district, isle* bring *the* (the United Kingdom, the Republic of Finland)
- an of-phrase brings *the* (the London of today)
- plural names take *the* (the Netherlands, the States, the Rocky Mountains)
- waters except lakes take *the* (the Thames, the Atlantic, the Mediterranean)
- map places take no article (Charing Cross, Stonehenge, Heathrow, Harrods, Central Park); leisure buildings take *the* (the British Museum, the Globe Theatre)
- parts of the globe take *the* (the Arctic, the Equator, the Middle East, the North Pole, the Tropic of Cancer)
- people take no article (even "25-year old Justin Bieber"); relatives (Mum, Uncle Ben); title + name (King William) vs "the King"
- plural family names take *the* (the Joneses)
- job title takes *the* ("Seymour Skinner, the headmaster")
- "the" = 'se kuuluisa' ("Do you mean **the** Clancy Wiggum?")
- "a" = 'joku' ("There's **a** Mr Szyslak to see you")

**Additional points from the AI page:**
- uncountables used as a "package": "a deep love", "a good knowledge of French" (YO S2025: **a knowledge**)
- "an angry Donald Trump" (a version of a person)
- body parts: "grabbed me by **the** arm, hit on **the** head"
- instruments: "plays the piano"
- the + singular for a class ("The tiger is endangered")
- headlines drop articles
- "many a student has failed" (singular verb)

**YO:**
- "Ainakin yksi artikkelikohta on mukana jokaisessa monivalintarakenteessa" (options "– / a / an / the")
- "Opettele sanat, jotka estävät artikkelinkäytön (this, these, that, those, lukusanat, own-sana)"
- "Earth vai the Earth? Kumpikin kanta sai natiiveja taakseen" (the exam board debated it): **accept both**
- K2026: "an eerie version"; S2025: "the subject", "a knowledge"; K2024: "in late 2022" (not "in the late 2022")

**Flags:**
- (a) **AI page, wrong:** "**That was a great courage**" (courage is uncountable: "great courage" / "a great deal of courage"); "so bad **a idea**" (should be *an idea*).
- (b) **AI page, misleading:** "quite = completely → quite a surprise". "Quite a surprise / quite a mess" means "a considerable, remarkable…", not "completely". Also "It takes a lot of courage" is filed under a + abstract noun, but *a lot* is a quantifier.
- (c) "Take off **the** jacket" as a body/clothing *the* example is not idiomatic; "take off **your** jacket" is standard when you mean the jacket you are wearing.
- (d) "a quite interesting book" is listed as the normal order; mainstream BrE strongly prefers "**quite an** interesting book" (a quite… is possible but less common).
- (e) "25-year old" in the deck lacks a hyphen; it should be **25-year-old** (his own adjective deck stresses hyphens).
- (f) "Was Louis Armstrong really the first man to land on the moon?" is a joke example (it was Neil Armstrong); don't reuse the fact.
- (g) "the internet (always with an article)" (Common-mistakes page) is basically right, but "internet" is article-less as a modifier ("internet access").

## 15. Possession: S-genetiivi, of-genetiivi, own (omistamisen ilmaiseminen)
Source: Omistaminen PDF; Opiskele-nämä page.

**Overview:** people take the s-genetiivi; things take the of-genetiivi. Maxx: "Porukoilla on usein tiedot tästä vähän sinnepäin ja Lautakunta tietää tämän." "Kaikki kokelaat eivät ole selvittäneet s-genetiiviä itselleen edes kymmenen vuoden enkunlukujen jälkeen… Heittomerkki kunniaan!"

**S-genetiivi** for people (Peter's brother), animals (the dog's name), places (London's sights) and time (today's paper).
- Singular takes 's (that boy's bag).
- A sibilant ending takes 's or just ' ("Charles's / Charles' mother", "ääntyy aina"); YO K2026: "Strauss's / Strauss'" both accepted.
- Regular plural takes ' only: "(the) boys' bikes" (boys / boys' sound the same, but writing must differ).
- Irregular plural takes 's: women's / men's magazines, the children's homework.
- Joint ownership puts the mark on the last name only (Tarzan and Jane's son); separate ownership puts it on each (Tarzan's and Jane's hobbies).
- A double genitive is replaced with of: "the world of Rowling's books".
- Elliptic place: "I have to go to the dentist's."

**Of-genetiivi** for relations between things: "The name of the band is…". It is also used for people ("the complete works of Kalle Päätalo"), for the + adjective groups ("the problems of the young / of the blind"), geographic names ("the Republic of Finland, the City of Oulu"), "the price of the ticket" (countable takes *the*), "the price of love" (uncountable has no article), "the owner of this car".

**Nationality adjective instead of a genitive:** "the Finnish flag"; "the Finnish President / the President of Finland / Finland's President".

**Possessives:**
- determiners: my, your, his, her, its, our, their ("I bought a house. Its price was high.")
- independent forms: mine, yours, hers, his, ours, theirs ("this bike is mine")
- no article or demonstrative directly before a possessive: "a friend of mine / that friend of yours / two of our friends"
- **own** only after a possessive or s-genetiivi ("my own business / Robert's own umbrella"); "Do you have **a room of your own**?"; Finnish *oma* is overused ("Stay in your room!" rather than "your own room")

**Noun + noun:** "the car door, the kitchen table, a street corner"; "my dog wagged its tail"; "a girls' night out"; "the meaning of life"; "the name of the game" (= pelin henki, an idiom).

**YO option sets:** "woman / woman's / women / women's", "its / their / the / a", "week / weeks / week's / weeks'".

**Error analyses (Appendix A):** its/it's is the most persistent apostrophe error (4 of 5 years); also plural apostrophes ("thing's", "hand's"), "Finlands education", "students mental health", "childrens'".

**Flags:**
- (a) **"a two days' holiday"** (slide 91) is non-standard. Use "**a two-day holiday**" or "**two days' holiday**" (no article). Never generate "a two days' holiday".
- (b) "pelin henki → the name of the game" is an idiomatic equivalent, not a literal translation; the exercise should not treat it as a genitive rule.

## 16. Quantifiers (paljoussanat)
Source: Paljoussanat PDF; Opiskele-nämä page; Common-mistakes page.

- **Step one:** decide whether the noun is C (countable: friends) or U (uncountable: time).
- **Little/few:**
  - few friends = "hyvin vähän" (negative); **a few** friends = "muutama kuitenkin" (positive)
  - little time / **a little** time
  - also *hardly any*, *not many/much*
  - very/too/so few, very/too/so little
  - **"only + vain artikkelillinen muoto": only a few / only a little** ("Lautakunta… ovat usean kerran kysyneet juuri tuota rakennetta")
- **Comparison:** few – fewer – the fewest; little – less – the least. "Nykyään myös: less friends."
- **Without a noun:** "There's little that we can do. / I have less to do. / Are you a little nervous?" (a bit / a little + adjective).
- **Much/many:** "yleensä kielloissa ja kysymyksissä"; in positive sentences use *very/too/so/as many/much*, or *a lot of / lots of / plenty of / a large number of* (C) / *a large amount of, a great deal of* (U) / **quite a few** (not "quite many").
- **a lot / a lot of:** "a lot of + pääsana, joten OF ei esiinny lauseen lopussa": "I like bananas a lot / I eat a lot of bananas."
- **more/most:** many – more – the most; much – more – the most; *many more friends / much more time*.
- **Most:** general "most students"; specified "**most of** the students in my class", "most of us". (YO options "For the most / Most / Most of / The most"; "for the most obvious reason".)
- **Phrases:**
  - quite a few people
  - the last few months, the next few days
  - at least ten
  - not in the least interested
  - to say the least
  - much to my surprise
  - much the same way
  - at the most
  - for the most part
- **YO items:** "couple / few / a couple / a few", "few / a few / many / many a"; "a couple of" (without *a* the point was lost, Appendix A); "million / million of / millions / millions of (x3!)"; K2024 "millions of people".
- **Other notes:**
  - "the whole + artikkelin saava sana (the whole week); the whole of + artikkeliton sana (the whole of June)"
  - "a few thousand / many thousands"
  - "the majority of"
  - the number of + singular verb / a number of + plural verb

**Flags:**
- (a) "Nykyään myös: less friends / the least friends" is informal; exercises should require **fewer/fewest** with countables.
- (b) "the whole of + artikkeliton sana" is incomplete: "the whole of **the** country / of the week" is also standard (the whole of + proper noun or a determined noun).
- (c) The Common-mistakes page says "majority" **always** needs *the* ("Väärin: Majority of students"). "*Majority of students*" is indeed wrong, but **"a majority of"** is standard ("A majority of voters…"). It also glosses "the majority" as "suuri osa"; it means more than half.

## 17. Pronouns, indefinite pronouns, agreement
Sources: scattered (Opiskele-nämä, Passiivi, Common mistakes, K2026 analysis, Appendix A/B).

- **Indefinite pronouns** ("Nämä veijarit ovat mukana joka kerta"): everybody, anyone, nowhere, something, some, any, no, every, each, all, both, each other… Option sets:
  - "any / any of / some / some of"
  - "any / few / some / something"
  - "anywhere / everywhere / nowhere / somewhere"
  - "each others / the other's / other's / the others"
  - "no / all / such / any"
  - "some / any / no / –"
  - K2026: "without **anyone** wondering" (*no* doesn't work after *without*)
- **No PowerPoint exists** ("On niin silppuinen asia…"). The linked file `indefinite-pronouns.ppt` is unread.
- **-one/-body pronouns are Y3:** "-one ja -body-loppuiset pronominit ovat Y3" (K2026 analysis: *has*). Spelling list: everyone/everything/something/nothing are one word.
- **it vs its vs it's** ("It's = se on / sillä on; its = sen").
- **Subject–verb agreement** (kongruenssi; K2026 *operas remain*, *shifts were reflected*; S2025 *effort has been*, *details have been*):

**Flags:**

## 19. Punctuation, capitalisation, sentence openers
Sources: Pilkun nuuskiminen page; Ehtovirkkeet; Relatiivi; Appendix A/B (spelling list).

- **Commas:** a fronted subordinate clause takes a comma ("If you ask nicely, I will help you"); a main + subordinate clause takes none. Non-defining relatives take commas, defining ones don't; *which* referring to a whole clause takes a comma.
- **2026 comma page ("Pilkku erottaa avaavan fraasin päälauseesta"):** 16 openers, each followed by a comma.
  - -ing ("Walking home, she realized…")
  - infinitive ("To avoid conflict, he stayed silent")
  - Having + 3rd form ("Having finished the exam, she relaxed")
  - past participle or adjective ("Exhausted, they cancelled the trip")
  - prepositional, place or time phrase ("In the middle of the night, the phone rang"; "That evening, …")
  - adverb or sentence adverb ("Suddenly, …", "Interestingly, …")
  - apposition ("A seasoned negotiator, she stayed calm")
  - cleft ("It was on the pier that Lennox kissed Lindsay")
  - negative inversion ("Never had she seen such chaos")
  - it/there
  - Exam advice: 1–2 such openers per text, clarity first, "Jos teksti kuulostaa paremmalta ilman pilkkua, jätä pilkku pois."
- **Capitalisation:** "I" is always capital (lower-case "i" appears in 4 of 5 years of error analyses); nationality adjectives are capitalised (Finnish, Swedish-speaking); the Sámi.
- **Apostrophes:** see §15. Also contractions (it's, you've, let's / let us): "Rakennemonivalinnassa ei ole kielenvastaisia vaihtoehtoja, mutta hyvä on tietää kussakin paikassa, mitä heittomerkki tarkoittaa." Option set: "you've / you're / you'd have / you'll be"; "let me / let's / let us / let us not".
- **Spelling list (vault):** at least, in fact, everyone, everything, something, nothing as one or two words.

**Flags:**
- (a) The **dangling openers** in this page's model sentences are listed in §11. The page's checklist also says "Onko jokaisen pilkun jälkeen selkeä päälause ja subjekti?", but its own examples fail that test because the subject is not the doer of the opening participle.
- (b) "Bang, the door flew open" and "Honestly" as an "interjection" are fine stylistically, but *honestly* is a sentence adverb.
- (c) The "the moon is always capitalised" claim (K2025 commentary, Appendix A) is wrong; dictionaries write "the moon" in lower case in ordinary use.
- (d) "Don't start a sentence with And" (K2026 commentary) is a style preference, not a grammar rule.

## 23. The 10 points most likely to trip up exercise generation (summary)

1. **Dangling participle openers.** The site's 2026 model sentences dangle; enforce the same-subject rule (§11).
2. **Terminology.** Maxx says *imperfekti*, not *preteriti*, and uses I/II konditionaali, pluskvamperfekti, lauseenvastike, perusmuoto (lyhyt/pitkä). Label tenses accordingly, or give both names (§2).
3. **Relative *that* is "only possible"** after superlatives, ordinals and *only*. Don't mark *who* wrong for people (§12).
4. **Backshift is optional** for still-true content, and mixed tenses in one sentence are fine, despite the "only past tenses" / "one side of the table" heuristics (§4, §8).
5. **in case ≠ if**, and **if + would/will** is possible in polite, insistence, refusal and future-result cases (§5).
6. **Modal possibility:** not "I can be right" or "He can have seen it"; *don't have to* = ei tarvitse, *mustn't* = kielto (§9).
7. **Articles:** a/an by sound; the article moves after *such/quite/what/half/too/as/how/so*; zero article with institutions, meals, transport, *last/next* = viime/ensi, *northern Finland*, sports results. Watch the AI page's errors ("a great courage", "so bad a idea") (§14).
8. **Compound measures and possessives:** *a seventeen-year-old student*, *a two-day holiday* (not "a two days' holiday"), *Strauss's/Strauss'*, *boys'/children's*, *its* vs *it's* (§13, §15).
9. **Quantifiers:** *fewer* (not *less*) with countables; *only a few / only a little*; *quite a few* (not quite many); *a couple of*; *millions of people* but *two million people*; *most* vs *most of the* (§16).
10. **Agreement and pronoun traps:** one of the + plural noun + singular verb; -one/-body = Y3; *every one of us* (two words); *anyone* after *without*; *people are*; *news is* (§17).

## 1. Articles
- `"As topic AI is not well known"` → **As the topic AI is…**: he says use the definite article (which topic? the one called AI), and that the article goes before all of the noun's modifiers ("I saw **a** funny old **man**") [S2024] ⚠

## 3. Subject–verb agreement and plural/singular
His core rule ("kongruenssi") repeats every year: a plural subject takes a plural verb. Finnish colloquial forms ("ihmiset haluu", "opet antaa") give the wrong model, so know good Finnish first ("ihmiset halua**vat**"). **This error appears in all 5 exams.**
- `"Only people who knows what they wants"` → **know… want** [S2024] **(R)**
- `"Teachers lets us take responsibility"` → **let**: "Ei ole rakettitiedettä" [S2024] **(R)**
- `"Perfectionist are usually unhappy"` → **Perfectionists are**: the verb is plural, so the head noun must be plural [S2024]
- `"In todays world jobs vanishes"` → **jobs vanish** [S2024] **(R)**
- `"Some people atleast tries"` → **at least try**: some people is plural [S2024] **(R)**
- `"Almost everybody close to me use AI"` → **uses** [S2024] (quoted; he comments only on the typo *colse*)
- `"So much have been corrected"` → **has**: *much* means uncountable, so the verb is singular [S2024]
- `"People who are perfectionist"` → **perfectionists** [S2024]
- `"Everybody want's success"` / `"most people want's"` → **wants / want** [K2025] **(R)**
- `"human race have not…"` → **has**: the human race is singular [K2025]
- `"There are many things that makes our school good"` → **make**: "On asioita, jotka TEKEVÄT", not "tekee" [S2025] **(R)**
- `"Students in our school is quite hardworking and excels"` → **are… excel** [S2025] **(R)**
- `"Trendy products that gives us…"` → **give** [S2025] **(R)**
- `"It affect everyhting"` → **affects**: it is Y3 [S2025]
- `"Most films has something…"` → **have**: "Suomen kieli ei saa toimia rakenteen mallina" [K2026] **(R)**
- `"In my opinion there is so many other art forms"` → **there are**: the plural head noun comes after the verb [K2026] **(R: there is + plural)**
- `"Is there other forms of life in the universe?"` → **Are there…**: interferenssi from "Onko…" [K2026] **(R)**
- `"Anyone who have watched…"` → **has**: -one/-body pronouns are Y3 [K2026]
- `"films does not require one… thus it is not art"` → **films do not… they are not art** [K2026]
- `"I want to know how people lives there"` → **live** [K2026] **(R)**
- `"There are other ways… that doesn't require"` → **don't** [K2026] **(R)**
- `"they represents modern day art"` → **represent** [K2026]
- `"The visual, verbal and musical elements keeps us entertained"` → **keep** [K2026]
- `"as the world changes, so does these these kind of things"` → **so do these kinds of things** [K2026]
- `"A person's actions doesn't define…"` → **don't**: "teot eivät määrittele" [S2026] **(R)**
- `"Those people just thinks…"` → **think** [S2026] **(R)**
- `"people with money helps and suprises people, that lives hard time"` → **help… surprise… who live (have) hard times** [S2026]
- `"What are the pros and cons… that has to be taken"` → **have** [S2026]
- `"Many people… Everyone of us thinkis"`: he comments only on *thinkis*; see the ⚠ in Pronouns.

**Irregular and wrongly formed plurals; "one of the + plural"**
- `"One of the biggest fear people have"` → **fears**: "yksi suurimmista peloista", one of many. Also *one of the fastest runners*, *best singers* [S2024] **(R)**
- `"one of the most talked about thing"` → **things** [S2024] **(R)**
- `"He his is one of the richest man"` → **men** [K2025] **(R)**
- `"one of the first country's"` → **countries** [S2025] **(R)**
- `"take one of the oldest form of art"` → **forms**: "Suomalaisilla tulee usein häikkää tässä" [K2026] **(R)**
- `"I knos some peoples"`, `"discussed with peoples"` → **people**: one person, many people; *peoples* = kansat [S2024, K2025] **(R)**
- `"meny student didin't learn nothing"` → **many students didn't learn anything** [S2024]
- `"It's a phenomena"`, `"This worldwide phenomena"` → **phenomenon** (pl. *phenomena*): "Yleinen väärinkäsitys nykyään" [K2025, S2025] **(R)**
- `"Why is this specie so stupid"` → **species** (sg = pl) [S2026]
- `"Ladies and gentleman"` (x2 essays) → **Ladies and Gentlemen** [S2025]
- `"multiples different causes"` → **multiple**: English adjectives don't take a plural ending [S2024]

## 5. Tenses and verb forms (incl. irregular verbs and -ed endings)
**Perfekti = have/has + 3. muoto (R: S2024, K2025)**
- `"I haven't have used it manytimes"` → **I haven't used it many times**: "Seiskalla opittu juttu" [S2024]
- `"I've been used AI"` → **I have used AI** [S2024]
- `"based on what I have saw"` → **have seen** [S2024]
- `"We are found secrets of happiness"` → **We have found**: don't translate "olemme löytäneet" piece by piece; identify the tense first [K2025]
- `"we are often think… you can not success… if we hadn't have friends"` → **we often think… cannot succeed… if we didn't have**: "aikamuotojen muodostaminen pitää osata" before anything else [K2025]
- `"human race have not learn"` → **has not learnt / learned** [K2025]

**Irregular verbs (R: S2024, K2025, K2026, S2026)**
- `"Finland is knowed by"` → **known**: know, knew, known. "Epäsäännölliset verbit kannattaa kerrata" [S2024]
- `"People have founded a way"` → **found**: find–found–found vs found–founded–founded (perustaa) [S2024]
- `"The dog runned"` → **ran**. Common offenders: drive, teach, throw, catch, lie (lie–lay–lain), lay (lay–laid–laid) [K2025]
- `"the animals which haded a home"` → **had** [K2025]
- `"it drived him crazy"` → **drove** [K2026]
- `"film industry has went towards"` → **has gone**. Dangerous verbs: catch, teach, drive, go, lie, lay [K2026]
- `"Unlike… ABBA sangs"` → **sang** (past) or **sings** (present, Y3 -s). The past tense has no 3rd-person -s [S2026]

**-ed endings and base forms (R: happen in K2026, S2026)**
- `"You just give AI the neede instructions"` → **needed**: a fine participle structure spoiled by the missing -ED [S2024]
- `"i downloadid police app"` → **I downloaded (a) police app**: past tense ending -ED [K2025]
- `"I hope I helpded you"` → **helped** [S2025]
- `"something scary is about to happend"`, `"if it happend once, it will happend again"`, `"Good things will happend"` → **happen**. Learners pick up "What happened?" from TV and treat it as the base form [K2026, S2026] **(R)**

**Other tense, verb-form and be/have issues**
- `"In the sixteens humankind sends a man on the moon"` → **In the sixties… sent… to the Moon**: use imperfekti for a past decade [K2025] ⚠ (see §21 on "Moon")
- `"Teachers are more freedom"` → **have**: be vs have, "I HAVE an ass vs I AM an ass" [S2024]
- `"Now I'll start this reply by answerin"` → **answering** [K2025]
- `"war industry has developed a lot more dangerous"` → **has become** [S2025]
- `"where using a PC came very familiar"` → **became**: Finnish "tuli tutuksi" showing through [S2025]
- `"Look at it successing"`, `"When you success"`, `"you can not success"` → **succeeding / succeed**: *success* is a noun; *to succeed (in + -ing)*, *successful* [K2025]
- `"companies that mass product stuff"` → **mass-produce**: to produce (verb) vs a product (noun) [S2025]
- `"to study"` → `"studing"` → **studying** [S2025]
- `"Is the blame on…"`: no grammar comment.

## 9. Relative pronouns
- `"One thing what should be improved"` → **which / that**: "Vanha Suomalaisten Sudenkuoppa". *What* already contains its antecedent (= se, mikä), and here the antecedent *thing* is right there [S2024] **(R)**
- `"Happiness is something what you can't buy"` → **that**: *what* contains the korrelaatti. After thing-ending pronouns use *that* ("ei muuten which-sanaa"). He says *what* and the limits on *that* come up often in the exam's grammar section [K2025] **(R)** ⚠ ("never which" is too strong)
- `"learn nothing from signs, what we see today"` → **signs that/which we see** (no comma: a defining clause, not a lisätietolause) [K2025]
- `"go buy their first names, witch would be…"` → **by… which** [S2025]
- `"…the positive outcomes making eco-friendly choices have"`: correct, but he recommends keeping **that** (…outcomes **that** making…) for clarity, since dropping it makes the sentence ambiguous [S2025]
- `"Methane is produced by cows in their stomach, which then is freed"`: he asks which word is the korrelaatti, methane or stomach. Avoid an ambiguous *which* [S2025]
- `"Which means more and more people…"` → don't start a sentence with relative *which*; attach it to the previous clause [S2025]
- `"Karma is something that's existence is debatable"` → **something whose existence** (whose works for people and things) or **the existence of which** [S2026]
- `"…, who mentally stable adult would donate…"` → **which** (interrogative: "kukapa henkisesti…"). Finnish influence [K2026] ⚠ (*what* is also natural)
- `"…wich leads to my other conclution"` → **which… conclusion** [S2026]; in his spelling list *wich → which* [S2026]

## 10. Word order and questions (inversion, do-support, indirect questions)
**Indirect / reported question = suora sanajärjestys (R: S2024, S2025, K2026, S2026)**
- `"AI doesn't really care is it ethical or unethical"` → **if / whether it is**: an epäsuora esitys with a reporting clause. A yes/no question is reported with IF/WHETHER plus straight word order (*whether* because there are two alternatives) [S2024]
- `"…how would I solve it"` (x2 essays) → **how I would solve it**: an indirect question has straight word order [S2025] **(R)**
- `"the experience of what would it be like living in Canada"` → **what it would be like**: inversion in a reported question makes the text hard to read [K2026] **(R)**
- `"whether or not should films be considered"` → **whether or not films should be considered** [K2026] **(R)**
- `"not so sure what is my real opinion"`: he says the example should have straight word order (**what my real opinion is**) [S2026] **(R)**

**Direct questions: do-support and inversion (R: K2025, K2026)**
- `"Why I think so?"` → **Why do I think so?**: Finnish isn't the model. Without DO the interrogative present goes wrong and the inverted order is missing [K2025]
- `"Why everyone searching for happiness…?"` → **Why is everyone searching…**: Finnish word order [K2025]
- `"But dose really happiness relay on these things?"` → **But does happiness really rely on…**: (does / really / rely) [K2025]
- `"Why you should select me?"` → **Why should you select me?**: a Finnish direct question has no inversion, but English does ("Varo suomen kielen SJ") [K2026]
- `"Is there other forms of life"` → **Are there** (see §3) [K2026]

**Inversion after negative adverbials**
- `"Not only gives a movie you an escape"` → **Not only does a movie give you**: inversion in the present needs the DO auxiliary [K2026]

**Wrong inversion (interference from Swedish)**
- `"For many centuries, have humans debated…"`, `"…multiple times, have I additionally questioned"` → **humans have debated / I have questioned**: Swedish V2 word order carried into English (interferenssi) [S2026]

## 11. Conditionals and wish
- `"If you want to think this poem is sad. Then it is."` → **If you want…, then it is.**: never leave a subordinate clause alone. SL + PL = a good virke, with a comma between them [S2024] **(R: fragments)**
- `"…because if you can't look at things as positive and you always [look] at things…"` → no main clause. IF opens a sivulause and *and* is coordinating, so "jos blaa ja blaa, NIIN…?" is left unanswered [K2025] **(R)**
- `"If the person needs success to feel like life is fulfilled or not."` → a lone subordinate clause [K2025] **(R)**
- `"Still, I think that if you are good person to other people. In the best scenario they notice…"` → **If you are a good person…, they will notice**: an if-clause needs a main clause [S2026] **(R)**
- `"For example, if you have just seen a homeless person… and after that you find 50$ on the ground."` → "NIIN? ENTÄ SITTEN? MIKSI PISTE?" [S2026] **(R)**
- `"If Donald Trump has the same effect… as Hitler in the early stages. If I was an American, I would not vote for him."` → the first if-clause is left hanging. He says the second sentence's tenses are right [S2026] ⚠ (was/were)
- `"…if we hadn't have friends"` → **if we didn't have friends** (2nd conditional) [K2025]
- `"Had Chalamet made his point…, he'd have more people"` → **he'd have had**: 3rd conditional with inversion [K2026]
- `"Climate change wouldn't be a problem if humanity wasn't could collaborate"` → **could collaborate / weren't unable to** [S2025]
- Other fragments: `"When we look at the sports."` (K2025), `"When you think about unexplored mysteries and problems that need an answer."` ("Eka virke pelkkä sivulause", S2025)

## 16. Conjunctions and linking words
- `"And many of these…, And even if…, And if we imagine…, And if space is…"` → don't begin sentences with *And*: it is a rinnastuskonjunktio that joins equal clauses within one sentence. "Ysi kääntyi kasiksi" [K2026] ⚠ (stated as a rule; see §21)
- `"Although, compulsory voting is becoming…"` → **Nevertheless / However**: *although* = vaikkakin and starts a clause, not a sentence adverb [S2026]
- Despite vs although: **despite + noun** ("Despite the rain"), **although + clause** ("Although it was raining") [S2026]
- `"There for it should be embraised"` (x3) → **Therefore… embraced** [S2026]
- `"Whenever karma comes up in a post nor a conversation"` → **or** [S2026]
- `"One the otherhand"` → **On the other hand** [S2026]
- Overuse of sentence-initial adverbials (*First and foremost, For instance, In addition, On the other hand, Moreover, Furthermore…, In conclusion*), all in one essay: a fine pattern, but the repetition is visible on one screen, so vary the structures [S2026]
- `"how can a rat cook better that a human"` → **than** [K2025]; `"…as unique… then your imperfections"` → **as** [S2024] **(R: than/then)**

## 21. Questionable or incorrect points (by mainstream standards: Cambridge, Oxford, Swan's *Practical English Usage*)
1. **"Everyone of us… — Alku on oikein (-one-loppuiset pronominit saavat of-määreen)" [S2026]: wrong.** Standard English writes **every one of us** as two words. *Everyone* (one word) cannot take an *of*-phrase (Swan; Cambridge Dictionary "everyone" usage note). His own S2026 spelling list (*every one → everyone*) covers only the stand-alone pronoun, so the two statements pull in opposite directions.
2. **"As topic AI…" → "As the topic AI…" [S2024]: doubtful.** The natural correction is **"As a topic, AI is not well known"** (*as* + role noun, indefinite or zero article), or "AI as a topic". His "which topic? the one called AI" reasoning doesn't match how *as a topic* works.
3. **"Thing-loppuisten pronominien kanssa käytetään that-sanaa (ei muuten which-sanaa)" [K2025]: overstated.** *That* is preferred after *something / everything / nothing / anything*, but *which* is possible and does occur (Swan §§ on relative *that*). It is a tendency, not a ban.
4. **"adjektiivin eteen ei artikkelia" [K2025]: misleading as a general rule.** The real rule is that a predicative adjective standing alone takes no article ("to be rich"). Articles normally do precede adjectives in a noun phrase ("a rich man"), as he himself teaches elsewhere ("a happy adventure", "a successful person").
5. **"Kuu isolla enkussa" [K2025]: overstated.** Oxford, Cambridge and most style guides write **the moon** in lower case in everyday use and capitalise *Moon* mainly in astronomical contexts or next to other proper names. Either is acceptable, so it isn't an error. The student's sentence also has *send… on the moon* where *to the Moon* is needed, which he doesn't mention.
6. **"Ja-sanalla ei juuri aloiteta virkettä" [K2026]: a style preference, not grammar.** Starting a sentence with *And* or *But* is grammatical (Swan; Oxford; Merriam-Webster). Repeating it four times is a legitimate style weakness, but the "rule" as stated is a school myth.
7. **"consider X as Y" [S2026]:** he half-concedes it, and correctly. *Consider X (to be) Y* is preferred, but *consider X as Y* appears in dictionaries (Oxford, Cambridge) and isn't an error. Marking it wrong four times would be harsh.
8. **"the happiness will spread" [K2025]:** the zero article fits the general concept. Here, though, the reference is to one specific person's good mood, so *the/that happiness* is defensible.
9. **"who mentally stable adult would donate" → "which" [K2026]:** the rhetorical-question determiner would more naturally be **what** ("What mentally stable adult would…?"). *Which* implies a choice from a known set.
10. **"comes alone with dirrerent kind of people" → "along" [K2026]:** the intended phrasal verb is **get along with** ("comes along with" means accompanies). His correction fixes only the spelling.
11. **"Change to an electric car" → buy/get [S2025]:** *switch to / change to an electric car* is normal English, so this is a nitpick rather than an error.
12. **"In the past couple years" → "couple of" [S2024]:** right for formal written BrE. *Couple* without *of* is common in informal AmE (Cambridge marks it informal/US), so it is a register issue more than a grammar error.
13. **"If I was an American, I would not vote" ("aikamuodot kohdillaan") [S2026]:** acceptable, but formal or exam-style writing prefers **If I were**. Swan calls *was* informal.
14. **"reoccuring" → "reoccurring" [S2026]:** the spelling fix is right, but the standard word for "happening repeatedly" is **recurring**. *Reoccur* means happen again once.
15. **"to see all that trouble" → "to go through the trouble of" [S2025]:** BrE standard is **go to the trouble of**. *Go through the trouble* is mainly American; both are acceptable.
16. **His S2026 spelling list:** *traveling* is correct American spelling, not an error (it is starred as a real word, but it sits under "wrong"). *wellbeing / well-being* are both standard. *dependant* is a valid BrE noun (he stars it as a real word in the wrong place, which is fine).
17. **"can not" bolded as wrong [K2025]:** *cannot* is the usual form, but *can not* isn't strictly an error (Swan; Oxford). The bold mainly targets *succ**ess*** there.

## 1. Articles (artikkelit)

Main source: abikurssi-goodies, "Maxx's Mini Grammar Corner: Artikkelit".

- Check articles separately as their own proofreading pass ("tarkasta erikseen artikkelit tekstistäsi").
- **Position:** the article comes before all of the noun's modifiers, with a few exceptions that "saattaa A-tason kokeessa olla": "**such** a nice song / **quite** an expensive watch / **too** expensive a watch (määre vaikuttaa artikkeliin)".
- **a/an:** "katso artikkelia heti seuraavaa sanaa": "**a** man, **an** old man".
  - FLAG (simplification): the choice depends on the next word's **sound**, not its letter: *an hour, an MP, a university, a European*. He does not say "letter", but students may read the rule that way.
- **Swedish analogy:** "en pojke (a boy), pojken (the boy), pojkar ( _boys), pojkarna (the boys)". The reasoning is roughly the same in both languages.
- **Heuristics for choosing an article:**
  - first mention is indefinite, later mentions are definite;
  - context, or there being only one of something, makes a noun definite;
  - use the definite article if you can assume the reader knows which referent you mean;
  - post-modification can make a noun definite at once;
  - "Laita laskettavalle sanalle artikkeli, jos ei ole syytä jättää sitä pois."
    - This is a sound heuristic: a singular countable noun needs a determiner. FLAG (minor): exceptions such as *by car, at school, in hospital (BrE)* are not mentioned here.
- **artikkeliharjoitus:** a humorous "Artikkelienpuutostauti … AADS (Acquired Article Deficiency Syndrome)". Article omission is "yleisin kirjoitelmia vaivaava vitsaus". The cure is study first, then checking in important places: "Meticulous, meticulous!"
- **Error examples** (erilaisten-virheiden): "I went to shop to buy new phone. → **I went to the shop to buy a new phone.**" Impact rated pieni–kohtalainen.
- **Celebrities (AI):** "'I saw a man' vs. 'I saw the man.' One is random, the other is specific." "'A Basilisk' means any old Basilisk. 'The Basilisk' means you're probably in trouble."
- **Spelling list:** proper names with *the*: "the Orient Express", "the Isle of Wight", "the French Communist Party", "the UN", "the Red Cross", "the Canary Islands", "the Middle Ages", "the Bible", "the Government", "the Samis". Names without *the*: "Lake Päijänne", "Aunt Lizzie", "President Stubb", "Lapland".
- **bad-grammar #41:** "I'm going to hospital" is listed as an omitted-article "bad grammar" item, with the note that it is standard in BrE. FLAG: it is not bad grammar at all in British English.

## 3. Agreement (kongruenssi)

Main sources: erilaisten-virheiden-merkitys, virheiden-rooli.

- Rule: "Yksi tekijä → yksikkömuotoinen verbi / Useampi tekijä → monikkomuotoinen verbi."
  - "The student is worried. / The students are worried."
  - "She has a problem. / They have a problem."
  - "Social media affects young people. / Social media platforms affect young people."
- **Typical errors:**
  - "People is worried about climate change → **People are…**"
  - "Young people has many opportunities → **have**"
  - "Social media have changed our lives → **Social media has changed our lives.**"
  - "One of the biggest problems are loneliness → **is**"
  - "The use of smartphones have increased → **has**"
  - "She have many friends → **She has…**"
- **Proximity trap:** a plural noun near the verb, but a singular head: "**One** of the biggest problems **is**…", "**The use** of smartphones **has**…".
- **Pikamuistisääntö:** ask "KUKA tai MIKÄ tekee?", then find the verb. Check is/are, was/were, has/have and the third-person -s ("He thinks. / People think.").
- **Ctrl+F proofreading:** search for *people, they, students, everyone, one, is/are, has/have, was/were* and look at the verb next to each hit. "**everyone** on englannissa yksikkö: Everyone is responsible. Everyone has a choice." Final check list: "people → are / have; everyone → is / has; one → is / has; students → are / have; he / she / it → verbin -s preesensissä".
- Severity: "kerran esiintyessään pieni – jatkuvasti toistuessaan merkittävä."
- **Example of a structural agreement / reference error:** "Young people who use social media is dangerous." (Who or what is dangerous?) Rated as a bigger impact because the reader must interpret.
- FLAG on **"Social media have changed our lives" marked wrong.** Both singular and plural are accepted. *Media* is historically plural, and Cambridge and Oxford list *social media* with singular or plural verbs. Even Maxx's own expressive-words page writes "The media sometimes exaggerate minor issues" (plural). It is safe to *recommend* singular for consistency, but plural should not be called an error.
- FLAG in virheiden-rooli: "Kongruenssivirheet, kuten **he has** / she have -tyyppiset ongelmat". *He has* is correct; presumably "he have" was meant. This is a typo in the source.
- **bad-grammar (AI) items:**
  - "There's two things" (#3): informal; avoid in writing.
  - "We was" (#26), "He don't" (#60, #100), "Y'all is" (#27).
  - "None of the students was late (instead of … were late)" (#85). FLAG: the explanation is backwards. The claim "None is technically plural" is wrong. Traditional prescriptive grammar wanted singular *none*; PEU says both singular and plural are normal (plural is more common informally). The "instead of" form is also given the wrong way round.
  - "Neither of the kids were at school (instead of … was)" (#91): called "grammatically incorrect". FLAG (overstated): PEU says a plural verb after *neither of* is common and acceptable in informal style; singular is more formal.
- Celebrities (AI): "One dude, one verb. Many dudes, many verbs." "He walks. They walk." "'He don't know' is right? (Spoiler: it's not.)"

## 8. Infinitive / -ing / that-clauses (verb patterns)

- **Verb + object + preposition/infinitive patterns** (sanaston, "Älä rakenna sanastoa pelkistä substantiiveista"):
  - "prevent **someone from doing**"
  - "accuse **someone of doing**"
  - "enable **someone to do**"
  - "result **in something**"
  - "distinguish **between two things**"
- **Error example:** "The government stopped people to use cars." Maxx asks whether this means "**The government stopped people from using cars**" or "**The government stopped to allow people to use cars**". Rated selvästi suurempi (the reader has to interpret).
  - FLAG (minor): the second "interpretation" is contrived. The real teaching point is *stop sb from doing* vs *stop to do* (stop in order to) vs *stop doing*. The page never spells out the stop + to-inf / -ing meaning difference.
- **Perusmuotokorvaus** (infinitive replacing a clause), from abikurssi:
  - After an ordinal, superlative or *next/last/only*: "Who was the first man **who landed on the moon**? → Who was the first man **to land** on the moon?" / "What's the best tool **to use** for this job?" / "Who's next **to be served**?"
  - In indirect questions: "Janet didn't know what **she should do** next. → Janet didn't know **what to do** next." / "I don't know if **I should laugh** or cry! → I don't know whether **to laugh** or cry!"
  - Add-on (implicit in the example, but not stated): *if* cannot be followed by a to-infinitive, so it must change to *whether*. This is worth stating explicitly.
- **Sanaston "Power English" units:** "prevent someone from doing", "be highly likely to", "contribute significantly to", "raise public awareness of", "meet the growing demand for", "gain access to".
- **Error:** "This may constitute into severe consequences" (wrong) vs "This may cause serious problems" (safe) vs "This may have serious long-term consequences" (better).
- bad-grammar (AI): "try and" (#30, #70) is called "technically incorrect in formal writing". FLAG (overstated): PEU treats *try and* as informal and idiomatic, not incorrect. Only the base form is possible (*tried and* is ungrammatical), which the page does not mention.
- bad-grammar #97: "I would rather you didn't go to the party (instead of I would rather you not go)" is called "nonstandard". **FLAG: reversed.** *Would rather + subject + past tense* ("I'd rather you didn't") is the standard form (PEU). *Rather you not go* is the marginal, mainly AmE subjunctive variant.

## 9. Participle clauses / lauseenvastikkeet

Main source: abikurssi, "Maxx's Grammar Corner: Lauseenvastikkeet". It appears twice, identically.

- Characteristics: "kirjallista tyyliä, kysytään eri tavoin ylppäreissä, huomataan kirjoitelmassa, tiivistää tyyliä, ei ole ainut tehokeino".
- **Rules:**
  1. An active clause's predicate shortens to the -ing form: "The people **who live here** are wealthy. → The people **living here** are wealthy."
  2. A passive clause's predicate shortens to the 3rd form (past participle): "The painting **that was stolen** last month… → The painting **stolen** last month has been found."
  3. "Yleisimmin lyhennetään sivulauseita, joilla on **sama subjekti** kuin päälauseessa."
  4. **Different subject: keep the subordinate clause's subject.** "As the rain **was** so hard, we couldn't go out. → The rain **being** so hard, we couldn't go out." This is the absolute construction, and the rule prevents dangling participles.
  5. Coordinate main clauses can also be shortened: "Jane worked very hard **and** earned a lot of money. → Jane worked very hard **earning** a lot of money."
  6. For time clauses, ask whether the actions are simultaneous or whether the subordinate action happened first.
     - Simultaneous: "Tom did his homework **while he listened** to music. → Tom did his homework **listening** to music."
     - Earlier: "When **he had done his homework**, he watched TV. → **Having done** his homework, he watched TV." ("Kysytään usein YO:ssa having + 3.muoto"). Or: "**After doing** his homework, he watched TV." ("Kysytään usein YO:ssa after + -ing-muoto").
  7. Infinitive replacement (see §8).
  8. When *be* is the only verb in the subordinate clause, it can be dropped: "When I **was** alone, I started to think. → **Alone**, I started to think."
- **FLAGS:**
  - **The "same subject" rule (3) is mixed up with reduced relative clauses (1–2).** For adverbial participle clauses, the implied subject must equal the main-clause subject, which is the dangling-participle rule. For **reduced relative clauses** the rule is different: the relative pronoun must be the *subject* of the relative clause. "I met a man **living** next door" is fine even though the main subject is *I*. The example "The people living here…" fits both rules only by coincidence.
  - **Rule 1 is overgeneralised for relatives.** -ing reduced relatives are not used for single completed past events (PEU; CGE). "The man **who won** the race" cannot become *"the man winning the race"* in a past-event sense. Stative verbs are fine ("people living here", "a box containing…").
  - **Dangling participles are never named explicitly.** Rule 4 implies the principle, but there is no warning example such as *"Walking down the street, the trees looked beautiful." Given how common this error is for Finnish writers, the omission is notable.
  - "Jane worked very hard **earning** a lot of money": standard punctuation needs a comma ("…very hard, earning a lot of money"), and the result reading differs slightly from "and earned". It is acceptable, but the bare form without a comma reads awkwardly.
  - "The rain being so hard": the absolute construction is formal or literary, and *heavy rain* is the collocation. Maxx himself marks "big rain → **heavy rain**" elsewhere, and "the rain was so hard" is not idiomatic. A better example would be "The rain being so heavy,…" or "It being late, we…".
- **Expressive words page** (prenominal participles and adjectives replacing relatives): "students who are motivated → **motivated students**"; "communities that are divided → **divided communities**"; "policies that are based on evidence → **evidence-based policies**"; "young people who are marginalised → **marginalised youth**"; "values that are deeply rooted → **deep-rooted values**"; "children who are well supported → **well-supported children**".
  - Minor: "workers who are exhausted by work → overworked workers" is redundant (*overworked employees* is given as well).
- **sanaston:** "lyhentynyt relatiivilause: commonly attributed to the treatment", in "The benefits **commonly attributed to the treatment** may have less to do with the treatment itself than with the surroundings **in which it is provided**."

## 10. Relative clauses (relatiivipronominit)

Main source: abikurssi, "Maxx's Grammar Corner".

- **Commas:** a defining clause (välttämätön/määrittelevä) has **no** comma; a non-defining clause (lisätietolause) **has** commas. Test: insert *muuten*. "Pilkunnuuskiminen ON tärkeää".
- **who** for people, **which** for things, **that** for both; **whose** for both (= jonka).
- "Aina kun olet laittamassa THAT, mieti kahdesti… Tätin kanssa pitää olla varpaillaan." That's "rider" (its list of demands):
  - (a) **no** non-defining clauses, so no comma before it;
  - (b) "Mun eteen EI tuu prepositiota!";
  - (c) "Superlatiivin kanssa that eikä mikään muu!";
  - (d) "-thing-loppuisten pronominien kanssa sitten vain that!";
  - (e) "Seuraavien sanojen kanssa vain that: all, little, much, few, first, last, only ja … kaikki järjestysluvut!"
  - "Lautakunta tietää tämän ja that-käyttörajoitteet ovat usein tapetilla ylppäreissä."
- **WHAT = "se, mikä"**; it has no antecedent ("viittaa itseensä, introvertti kun on"). The typical Finnish error: "The present **what** I got from Granny was great." The correction: "The present **which/that** I got from Granny was great." Correct use: "**What** you're saying is true!"
- **Omission:** the relative pronoun can be dropped when the clause keeps its own subject and predicate (i.e. object relatives): "The present **I GOT** from granny was great."
- **Sentential relative:** when the antecedent is the whole preceding clause, use **which**: "I can speak Swedish well, **WHICH** is nice!" (Swedish *vilket*). Spelling: not "witch/wich".
- bad-grammar #31: "She's the girl that won the contest (instead of … who won)" is called misuse. FLAG: *that* for people in defining clauses is standard (PEU, CGE). Maxx's own rule says "that käy molemmille", so the AI list contradicts his teaching.
- **FLAGS on Maxx's that-rules (c)–(e): stated as absolute, but they are preferences.**
  - After superlatives, *all, only, first*, etc., *that* is **preferred**, especially for things. For people, **who is normal and correct**: "the first man **who** walked on the moon", "the only person **who** understood", "the best player **who** ever lived" (PEU: who is common after superlatives and *first/last/only* for people).
  - After *-thing* pronouns, *that* is usual, but "something **which**…" also occurs (PEU says *that* is "more common").
  - In a YO multiple-choice item, *that* would be the safe key, but students should not mark *who* wrong in these contexts.
  - Rule (a) is correct. Rule (b) is correct for "preposition + that" (*the house in that…), though a stranded preposition with *that* is fine ("the house that I live in").
  - The omission rule is correct but incomplete: omission happens only in **defining** clauses and never for a subject relative (except in *there is* constructions in speech).
- bad-grammar #4 "Who vs. Whom": "Who are you talking to? (instead of Whom are you talking to?)". FLAG: "Who … to?" is standard. "Whom are you talking to?" is a stilted hybrid; the formal version is "To whom are you talking?".
- erilaisten: use Ctrl+F on **which** to check whether sentences have become too long.

## 11. Word order / inversion / emphasis (cleft, fronting)

- **Pseudo-clefts (wh-clefts), all AI-sourced in rakenteita but approved by Maxx:**
  - "What matters most is …", "What really matters is …", "What is crucial / striking / surprising / clear is …", "What becomes clear is …", "What stands out is …"
  - "What particularly impressed me was her voice." (from the YTL muunnostehtävä sample; the transformation target "I …" → *I was particularly impressed by her voice*)
  - "What matters is not speed but direction." "What we need is not more information but better judgment."
- **"The X is that…" frames:** "The irony is that technology meant to save time often wastes it." "The paradox is that more choices often make people less satisfied." "The bottom line is that the system is too expensive." "The elephant in the room is that nobody trusts the new manager."
- **Not X but Y:** "The problem is not technology but how we use it." / "People support the reform not because it is perfect, but because it is necessary."
- **Inversion after not only** (english-structures #8): "Not only is my phone battery low, but it's also judging my life choices." "Not only is my cat spoiled, but he also acts like he pays rent." Non-inverted variant (rakenteita): "Technology **not only** changes work, **but also** shapes society."
- **so-inversion:** "As technology grows, **so do** expectations."
- **Comparative correlative:** "**The more** we rely on technology, **the more** vulnerable we become." "The more choices people have, the less satisfied they sometimes feel." "The sooner governments act, the better." "The longer we ignore the problem, the harder it becomes to solve."
- **Fronting of adjectives:** "Alone, I started to think." "Worse still, nobody seems responsible." "Better still, the policy saves money."
- **so … that / such:** "She was so tired that she fell asleep standing up." "He was so late that they started without him." Also "such a nice song" (see §1).
- Muunnostehtävä sample: "lauseen alku on muutettu ja loppu tuotetaan sen edellyttämällä tavalla".
- erilaisten: "väärä sanajärjestys voi jo tehdä lauseesta kömpelön tai epäselvän" (virheiden-rooli).
- FLAG (rakenteita, "Short Punch Echoes"): "**Still today**, the issue divides opinion" is Finnish-influenced (*vielä tänäkin päivänä*). Natural English says "**To this day** / **Even today**…". Also, "One thing is clear …" should be followed by a colon (as the later example does: "One thing is clear: change is coming.").

## 14. Adjectives / adverbs / comparison

- **bad-grammar intro, "superlative of adverbs of manner"** (pasted AI reply, with Maxx's comment). Stated "traditional rule":
  - "Adjectives: Always take the in the superlative."
  - "Adverbs: If the adverb ends in -ly, the is required. She ran **the most gracefully** of all."
  - "Short adverbs (fast, hard, late): no the. She ran fastest of all. (not the fastest)"
  - The AI says "He ran the fastest" is common. Maxx: "**No way, José!**"; his "personal mission" is to omit *the* with adverb superlatives.
  - **FLAG (significant):** mainstream grammars do not support the stated rules.
    - PEU and CGE: with adverb superlatives *the* is **optional** ("Who can run (the) fastest?"), and *the* is very common and standard in both speech and writing.
    - The claim that *-ly* adverbs *require* the is false: "She ran most gracefully of all" is fine, and *the* is optional there too.
    - "Adjectives always take the" has exceptions: predicative comparison of one thing with itself ("The lake is **deepest** here"), possessive determiners ("my best friend"), and *most* meaning "very" ("a most interesting book").
    - Maxx's stricter preference is acceptable as a stylistic choice but should not be taught as a rule or used as a multiple-choice key.
- bad-grammar (AI):
  - "He sings good → well" (#1).
  - "She talks real slow → really slowly" (#16, #73).
  - "the most easiest" (#9) and "more better" (#50, #82): double comparatives.
  - "so fun vs such fun" (#45).
  - "That's way too expensive (instead of far too)" (#52): informal intensifier.
  - "I didn't know it was that hard (instead of so hard)" (#51). FLAG: "that hard" is standard (*that* as a degree adverb); it is informal, not bad grammar.
  - "I'm good vs I'm well" (#92): "Good is an adjective, and well is an adverb". FLAG: in "I'm well", *well* is an **adjective** (= healthy), so the given reason is wrong.
  - "She's taller than me (instead of She's taller than I [am])" (#25), headed "Subject Pronoun Instead of Object Pronoun After 'Than'". FLAG: the heading is reversed (it is the object pronoun used instead of the subject pronoun). Also, "than me" is the normal standard form (PEU); "than I" is formal, and "than I am" is the natural careful alternative.
  - "taller then me" (#40): spelling.
- sanaston: "less to do with X than with Y" is a comparison structure. Word families: "rapid **expansion** / **expanded** rapidly / an **expanding** industry" (choose the right word class).

  - "People are interested **about** politics → **in**"
  - "discuss **about** the problem → **discuss the problem**"
  - "Social media makes problems **to** young people → **Social media causes problems for young people.**"
  - "do a decision → **make a decision**"; "big rain → **heavy rain**". Collocation errors are rated kohtalainen: "Kollokaatiovirheet voivat olla yllättävän merkittäviä".
- **Prepositional verbs and expressions** (sanaston): "account for", "result in" (= johtaa johonkin) vs "result from" (= aiheutua jostakin), "address a problem", "pose a threat", "meet a demand", "be attributed to", "in response to", "pave the way for", "play a role in", "come at a cost", "be subject to change", "take something for granted", "show no sign of slowing", "stem from", "contribute to", "distinguish between".
- **Verb–noun collocations** (sanaston): "draw a conclusion, reach an agreement, raise concerns, pose a risk, meet a need, gain access, conduct research, address an issue, challenge an assumption, undergo treatment". These distinguish natural English from English translated directly from Finnish.
- **Learning method:** learn words in context. Weak: "allocate – myöntää". Better: "**allocate resources** – The government allocated additional resources to education." Also "compelling evidence", "under financial constraints", "conditions deteriorated rapidly".
- **Exam tip** (monivalinta, the vault and other pages): "Aina tärkeät prepositiot HUUTAVAT kertausta. Esimerkiksi rakennekokeessa voit pelata pois vaihtoehtoja hyvällä prepositiotietämyksellä."
- **Celebrities (AI):** "On time or in time? Tiny words, massive consequences."
- **Muunnostehtävä samples (YTL type):**
  - "Will you take care of my flat while I'm away? (Verbin muutos: LOOK)", i.e. *look after*;
  - "I don't want to be treated like a fool anymore. (Adjektiivin muutos: SICK)", i.e. *sick of being treated*;
  - "He is not sure whether to go or not. (Sanonta: MINDS)", i.e. *in two minds about whether to go*.
- **bad-grammar (AI):**
  - "Where are you at?" (#8) is labelled "Preposition Stranding". FLAG: mislabelled. This is a redundant *at*. Preposition stranding ("Who are you talking to?") is standard English; the list also implies elsewhere (#4) that it is wrong.
  - "off of" (#32); "on accident → by accident" (#49).
  - "I got it for free! (instead of I got it free!)" (#57). FLAG: *for free* is standard.
  - "I'll meet you at the hospital (instead of in the hospital…)" (#84). FLAG: *at* is standard for a place as a point or meeting place.
  - "because weather → because of the weather" (#19).
- Sanaston false friends: "eventually ≠ mahdollisesti; actual ≠ ajankohtainen; comprehensive ≠ ymmärtäväinen; sympathetic ≠ sympaattinen kaikissa yhteyksissä; fabric ≠ tehdas".
- ENA8 (minor slips in Maxx's own text): "privacy **on** their free time" → *in* their free time; "non-sequencial bills" → *non-sequential*.

## 17. Linking words & sentence adverbs (lauseadverbit, sidosteisuus)

- **lauseadverbit page.** "Lauseadverbit ovat voimaenglantia. Niillä voit osoittaa, miten seuraava lause liittyy muuhun… Ne kertovat kirjoittajan suhtautumisesta asiaansa. Niitä on englannissa helppo käyttää: **ne aloittavat virkkeen ja ne erotetaan pilkulla** muusta lauseyhteydestä." There is a moderation warning: "niiden yletön käyttö ei ole suositeltavaa".
  - **List of 56:** Admittedly, All things considered, As a general rule, As far as we know, Astonishingly, Broadly speaking, By and large, Characteristically, Clearly, Coincidentally, Conveniently, Curiously, Disappointingly, Equally, Essentially, Explicitly, Even so, Eventually, Fortunately, Fundamentally, Furthermore, Generally speaking, Interestingly (twice), Ironically, In essence, In general, In particular, In practice, In reality, In retrospect/hindsight, In theory, In view of this, Naturally, On balance, Obviously, On reflection, Overall, Paradoxically, Potentially, Predictably, Presumably, Primarily, Probably, Remarkably, Seemingly, Seriously, Significantly, Specifically, Surprisingly, Theoretically, To all intents and purposes, Typically, Ultimately, Understandably, Undoubtedly, Unfortunately, With hindsight.
  - **FLAG (placement rule stated as absolute):** sentence adverbs are not always initial with a comma. Many sit in **mid-position** without commas: "He will **probably** come", "This is **clearly** wrong", "It was, **admittedly**, expensive". *Probably* in particular is far more natural mid-sentence. "Probably, …" at the start is marked in British English (PEU: *probably* usually goes in mid-position). Also, *Explicitly, Eventually, Potentially, Primarily, Specifically* are mostly not sentence (disjunct) adverbs, so a sentence-initial "Eventually, …" is fine but "Explicitly, …" or "Potentially, …" is odd.
  - **FLAG (glosses):**
    - "Admittedly = Eittämättä": *admittedly* = myönnettäköön (that), while *eittämättä* = undeniably.
    - "Predictably = Ennustusten mukaan": *predictably* = kuten arvata saattoi, as the second gloss correctly says; *ennustusten mukaan* = according to forecasts, which is wrong.
    - "Conveniently = Mukavasti" is weak.
    - "In view of this = Huomioonottaen" lacks an object.
    - Typos: "Käytännölliesti", "Vime kädessä", "Kaikenkaikkiaan".
- **rakenteita (AI) connectors:**
  - Contrast: "but, yet, still, however, even so, at the same time, on the other hand, that said, even then, all the same, nevertheless, nonetheless, despite this, in spite of this, instead, rather, by contrast, in contrast, worse still, better still, if anything, after all".
  - Starters: "One could argue that… It is often argued that… It is worth noting that… It is important to remember that…". Maxx's own warning here: "**Varovasti näiden yllä olevien kanssa. Eivät nuo hirveästi näkökulmaa esitykseen vielä tuo.**"
  - Two-sentence contrast: "The idea sounds attractive. Yet the reality is more complicated."
  - FLAG (punctuation/register): "Some see technology as progress, others see it as a threat." This is a comma splice in formal writing; use a semicolon or "…progress, while others…" (the next example correctly uses a semicolon).
  - FLAG: "From a broader view, the policy makes sense" is unidiomatic; the idiomatic forms are *from a broader perspective* or *taking a broader view*. "Remote work reflects a revealing trend" is an odd collocation.
  - FLAG: "In the bigger picture, …" is used repeatedly as a sentence adverbial. It is acceptable but less idiomatic than "Looking at the bigger picture," or "In the grand scheme of things,".
- sanaston: contrast vocabulary "whereas, while, despite, nevertheless, in contrast, on the other hand, rather than"; cause/effect "cause, lead to, result in, stem from, contribute to, trigger".
- abikurssi: the YTL grading criteria emphasise **sidosteisuus** (cohesion). There are links to Maxx's sidosteisuus article and to digital exercises on konjunktiot / linkkisanat.
- erilaisten Ctrl+F tip: "Jos käytät usein **because**, tarkista, onko jokaisessa virkkeessä myös varsinainen päälause." Fragment examples: "Because social media which is very popular among young people and many of them spending several hours there every day." / "One reason why people are worried about this because children are using phones too much." Both are rated **suuri**.

## 18. Punctuation / capitalisation / spelling

- **Relative clause commas:** see §10.
- **Sentence adverbs:** a comma after an initial adverb (§17).
- "Let's eat, Grandma." vs "Let's eat Grandma." (AI pages, repeated).
- **Run-on sentences** (erilaisten): a single long "Nowadays young people use social media which has many advantages but there are also disadvantages which…" sentence. Better: "**Social media has many advantages, but it also has serious drawbacks. Young people may spend several hours a day online. This can affect their well-being, so the issue deserves attention.**" Impact: **suuri**. "Pisteitä ei saa virkkeen pituudesta." rakenteita: "virkkeen pituus EI ole arviointikriteeri… Useimmiten parin, joskus ehkä kolmen lauseen virke on riittävä."
- **Spelling and capitalisation list** (oikeinkirjoitus):
  - Commonly confused pairs: taught/thought, hole/whole, witch/which/with, width, wealthy, although, because, development, hobby, conscience, disappointed, embarrassed, principal/principle, colleague, exciting, except, effect (noun) / affect (verb), beautiful, thoroughly, develop, surprise, accommodation, wherever, jewellery, immediately, jealous, quite/quiet, successful/succeed, guest/guess, question, storey/store/story, loose/lose, especially, environment.
  - **Capitals:** Christmas Eve, Finnish / a Finn, Lapland, July, September, Roman Catholic, the Isle of Wight, the French Communist Party, Aunt Lizzie, God Almighty, the Second World War, President Stubb, the Government, the Bible, the Red Cross, the Canary Islands, the Middle Ages, Jewish / a Jew, the UN.
  - Typos to Ctrl+F (erilaisten): enviroment, becouse, goverment; than/then, their/there, affect/effect, lose/loose.
  - **FLAGS:**
    - "lappalainen (henkilö) = **Lapp**": the term is considered pejorative or dated. Use *Sámi* / *a Sámi person*.
    - "saamelaiset = **the Samis**": the usual form is *the Sámi* (or *Saami*), uninflected.
    - "ruotsia puhuva suomalainen = a Swedish speaking Finn": the compound needs a hyphen, "a **Swedish-speaking** Finn".
    - "loppu = finish": *end* is the usual noun; *finish* is mainly for races.
    - "kerros = storey": BrE spelling (AmE *story*). Note this for students mixing varieties.
    - "hallitus = the Government": a capital is optional (*the government* is common).
- Celebrities (Maxx persona, AI): "DO care about spelling."

## 19. Register / style

- **ENA8 definition (quoting OPH and Wikipedia):** register is a language variety tied to situations and user groups; registers differ in vocabulary, grammar and syntax. Formality brings fuller pronunciations and forms; reductions, assimilations and colloquialisms drop out.
- **Informal contractions** (gonna, wanna…) are taught for "street cred" in speech (ENA8 links). bad-grammar #28 and #68 note *gonna/gotta* are informal and should be kept out of writing.
- **"Set the bar lower in speech, higher in writing"** (Celebrities, Maxx persona). Virheiden-rooli: in conversation, communication is negotiated; "Ylioppilaskokeen kirjoitelma on kuitenkin eri tilanne… Tekstin on toimittava itsenäisesti."
- **Go with what you know:** "Sensorit bongaavat maksullisten sanasto-ohjelmien paperinmakuiset erikoissanat… (esim. plethora/myriad-sanat saavat jo karvat pystyyn)" (abikurssi, taalla-…). "If in doubt, leave it out." "Kirjoituksissa ei kannata ottaa suuria riskejä."
- **Clichés and idioms:** "Kliseet ovat enkussa valmiita pitkiä Legoja, jotka yhtenä tyylikeinona ovat vakuuttavia, mutta ainoana keinona näyttävät tältä. Joten maltti on jälleen kerran valttia."
- **Conciseness (1300-character limit):** nominalisation ("the fact that many people are unemployed → **unemployment**"), adjectives instead of relative clauses (§9), "implied idioms" ("The silver lining is…", "The last straw was…", "…only the tip of the iceberg", "a double-edged sword", "a blessing in disguise", "a vicious circle", "a fine line between…").
  - FLAG: "**The last straw that broke the camel's back**" is given as the "full saying". The proverb is "**the straw that broke the camel's back**"; *the last straw* is the derived phrase. The two are conflated.
  - FLAG (minor): "#13 A turning tide": the example "Public opinion may finally be turning" does not use the phrase. "A sign of the times = Etätyö on **ajan henki**": *ajan merkki* is closer. "A losing battle = hävitty taistelu": *toivoton taistelu* is closer (the battle is not yet lost).
- **english-structures register warning (not stated on the page):** most of the ~200 AI templates are meme or social-media register ("X has left the chat", "Nobody: / Absolutely nobody:", "I have trust issues because autocorrect", "Not sure if lazy or just conserving energy"). The page itself says they are "gold for humour, social media, and everyday speech". FLAG for YO use: they are **unsuitable for formal YO essays**. "because + noun" ("because autocorrect") is nonstandard in writing, and dropped-subject fragments are informal.
  - Usable in essays: "There's no such thing as X", "X doesn't mean Y" ("Being busy doesn't mean being productive"), "It's not about X, it's about Y", "X is to Y what A is to B", "X is one thing, but Y is another", "the difference between X and Y is Z", "so … that".
  - **Garbled AI examples to discard:**
    - "A workout is like a jog, but with more sweat on steroids"
    - "Cold pizza is like revenge—best served cold"
    - "Procrastination is laziness, only faster"
    - "A day off is a reminder to work harder next week in disguise"
    - "That test was the hardest thing since calculus discovered the meaning of pain"
    - "Success is work, but only if you're willing to fail first"
    - "A dream is where motivation goes to die"
    - "#34 'X so nice, they did it twice'": the heading doesn't match the examples ("named it twice")
    - "Pop quizzes? That's just teacher revenge in disguise" / "A group project? That's just one person working…" (frame not followed)
- **rakenteita "Simple Formulas for Wisdom Sentences"** (AI): "X solves Y, but creates Z", "X comes at a cost", "X without Y is dangerous", "Talking is easy; listening is harder." These are acceptable. The claim that "Students who master just these five frames can already produce very natural C1-level sentences" is AI hype; treat it with caution.
- **Writing process** (rakenteita, Maxx's own): the reader is not me; justify every sentence; be critical of your own text.

## 20. Error weighting in YO grading (YTL / Lautakunta)

**erilaisten-virheiden-merkitys:**
- The main criterion is **viestinnällisyys**: "Mitä kokelas on halunnut ilmaista, ja kuinka hän on ilmaisussaan onnistunut?" Correctness, vocabulary, structures and error severity "tukevat ennen kaikkea tätä pääkriteeriä". In the YTL descriptors, levels differ mainly by "**haittaavatko virheet viestin välittymistä vai eivät**".
- The principle: "Virheen vakavuus ei riipu vain siitä, mikä kielioppisääntö rikkoutuu. Ratkaisevaa on, mitä virhe tekee lukijalle." Formula: "**virheen vakavuus = kuinka paljon lukija joutuu tekemään töitä**".
- **Severity scale:**

| Error type | Examples | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Typo | enviroment, becouse, goverment | lähes harmiton |
| Small grammar error, clear meaning | people is, she have, since three years | pieni, if occasional |
| Article / preposition / form | to shop, depends of, interested about | pieni–kohtalainen |
| Agreement | (see §3) | one-off: small; repeated: significant |
| Wrong word / unidiomatic | makes problems to, do a decision, big rain, discuss about | kohtalainen |
| Meaning briefly obscured | stopped people to use, "Young people who use social media is dangerous" | selvästi suurempi |
| Over-long sentence | (see §18) | suuri |
| Broken sentence structure | fragments | suuri |
| Wrong key idea | increase/decrease, can/can't, should/shouldn't, more/less, before/after, although/because | erittäin suuri |
| Unrecoverable meaning | "this thing … they … it … that way" | erittäin suuri |

- "YTL korkeimmassakin pistekategoriassa sallitaan yksittäisiä kielellisiä virheitä, kun ne eivät vaikeuta viestin välittymistä."
- Point levels, as Maxx summarises the YTL criteria:
  - **84 p**: "jonkin verran virheitä, kunhan ne eivät olennaisesti vaikeuta viestin välittymistä";
  - **74 p**: "osa virheistä voi jo vaikeuttaa sitä";
  - **64 p**: "virheitä on useita ja osa niistä todella vaikeuttaa viestin ymmärtämistä".
  - (Unverified against the current YTL document; it is not in these files.)
- "Hyvä kirjoitelma ei ole virheetön kirjoitelma. Hyvä kirjoitelma on teksti, jossa viesti kulkee lukijalle mahdollisimman esteettömästi." "Yksinkertainen mutta hyvä virke… on parempi kuin kunnianhimoinen mutta hutera virke."
- **Proofreading method:**
  1. First pass for comprehension, not commas ("Jos joudut itse lukemaan virkkeen kaksi kertaa, se on varoitusmerkki").
  2. Split long sentences, clarify pronouns, replace words you are unsure of, add missing verbs.
  3. Use Ctrl+F for your own risk words.
  4. Keep a personal list of your **5–10** most common error types.
  5. Checklist: "Onko jokaisessa virkkeessä verbi? Onko subjekti ja verbi samaa lukua? Ovatko aikamuodot järkeviä? Ovatko pronominien viittaukset selviä? Onko jokin virke liian pitkä?"

**virheiden-rooli:**
- "Ylioppilaskirjoitelman tärkein arviointiperuste on viestivyys… Kirjoitelma ei ole pelkkä kielioppikoe, jossa jokainen pieni virhe mekaanisesti vähentää pisteitä. Yksittäinen virhe harvoin pilaa hyvää tekstiä."
- "Ongelma syntyy silloin, kun virheitä on paljon tai ne ovat sellaisia, että lukeminen hidastuu."
- Hierarchy: a typo is often harmless; word order can make a sentence clumsy or unclear; structural deficits ("virheelliset sivulauseet, epäselvät viittaussuhteet tai liian pitkät lauseet") hinder reading more; agreement "pitää olla kunnossa".
- On AI: practise "ilman tekoälyä, ilman automaattikorjausta ja ilman käännösohjelmaa". The exam reveals "sanasto, lauserakenteet, aikamuodot, artikkelit, prepositiot, pilkut ja tekstin kokonaisrakenne".
- "pikkuasiat … ratkaisevat tärkeässä paikassa, onko tuotos keskitasoinen, hyvä vai erinomainen."
- "Kirjoitelma on maksimissaan … 1300 merkin mittainen… If in doubt, leave it out."

**abikurssi (exam-level tips):**
- The essay is "vain kolmasosa kokeesta (max. 99p/299)"; "useimmat tehtäväkohdat ovat 3 pisteen arvoisia", so "pienet asiat ovat suuria asioita". "Olen nähnyt ysin kirjoitelmalla valahdettavan magnaan."
- **Task compliance deductions** (YTL guide p. 32, quoted): "Puheessa täytyy alussa puhutella yleisöä ja lopussa kiittää; kirjeessä on oltava aloitus ja lopussa nimi. Jos nämä puuttuvat, tehdään pistevähennys." A real case: 3 of 12 students lost points for not following instructions. "Lue ohjanta, ymmärrä ohjanta ja noudata ohjantaa."
- Peer assessment by YTL criterion order: viestinnällisyys → sisältö ja rakenne → kielellinen laajuus ja tarkkuus.
- Prediction for S 2026: the listening reaction section may pause; an all-English exam and an oral part are coming "mutta ei vielä".

**Exam format:**
- YTL: the language exam measures kuullun ymmärtäminen, luetun ymmärtäminen, kirjallinen tuottaminen and **sanaston ja rakenteiden hallinta**. Possible task types include monivalinta, aukkotehtävät and vapaat tuottamistehtävät. Drafts on paper are not graded. Maxx: "Lue ohje, ymmärrä ohje ja noudata ohjetta!"
- Muunnostehtävä: YTL describes it as a possible type ("aikamuodon muutos, sivulause lauseenvastikkeeksi, erillisten lauseiden yhdistäminen…"). Maxx (muunnoslauseita): it "ei ole esiintynyt kertaakaan sellaisenaan" but is useful practice.
- The vault: "Eihän enkussa hirveästi ole taivutusta… ns. säännötkin usein votavat [sic] kuin seula… Mikään sanastokikkailu ei pelasta sitä elintärkeää kielen sujuvuutta, jos kielioppi kinnaa."
- sanaston: the exam's core level is "vahvan B2-tason ja C1-tason alun"; the main difficulty is paraphrase recognition ("demand continues to grow → no sign of slowing").

## 21. Consolidated flags

**AI-generated or pasted content**
1. **bad-grammar**: a pasted chatbot reply ("You're right that traditional grammar books say…") plus an AI list of 100 items with ≈15 duplicates (#2/47/75, #5/61, #14/77, #15/90, #21/55/89/96, #30/70, #44/93, #50/82, #56/81, #18/46…).
2. **celebrities-…grammar**, **english-structures**, **rakenteita-tiiviiseen**: explicitly ChatGPT ("Chatty Gepetto").
3. **expressive-words**, **sanaston-ja-rakenteiden**, **erilaisten-virheiden**: likely AI-assisted, with no explicit attribution. Sanaston has an orphan reference: "Molemmat ovat … kokeita" with no antecedent.

**Wrong or reversed claims in bad-grammar**
- #97: "I would rather you didn't" is called nonstandard. It is the standard form.
- #85: "None is technically plural". The claim is false, and the corrected form is reversed.
- #25: the heading says subject/object the wrong way round, and "than me" is standard.
- #8: "Where are you at" is mislabelled as preposition stranding (stranding itself is standard).
- #6: "Lack of Past Tense" is a mislabel; it is the were-subjunctive.
- #10: "known antecedent" is a mislabel, and singular *they* is standard.
- #92: "well is an adverb", but in "I'm well" it is an adjective.
- #42: habitual *be* ("She be working") is glossed as "is working"; it means "usually works".
- #48 ("He's more of a leader than I of a follower") and #95 ("Could of you helped me?") are invented nonsense.
- #99 ("Every information") and #66 ("much friends") are learner errors, not native "bad grammar".

**Standard usage wrongly labelled "bad grammar"**
- "going to hospital" (BrE), "the girl that won", "these ones", "for free", "at the hospital", "that hard", "Can I have…?", "have got", "Who are you talking to?", "try and" (informal but idiomatic), "Neither of the kids were" (acceptable informally), and "Everyone … their".

**Superlative adverbs (bad-grammar intro, endorsed by Maxx's "No way, José!")**
- The claims "-ly adverbs require *the*" and "adjectives always take *the*" are false.
- With adverb superlatives *the* is optional and "the fastest" is standard (PEU, CGE).

**Maxx's own rules**
- That-rules after superlatives, *-thing*, *all/only/first*/ordinals are stated as "only that". They are only a preference; *who* is correct for people ("the first man who…", "the only person who…").
- Lauseenvastikkeet: the "same subject" rule is mixed up with reduced relatives (the real rule is that the relative pronoun is the subject).
- -ing reduction of relatives is overgeneralised; it cannot express single completed past events.
- Dangling participles are never named or warned against explicitly.
- "Jane worked very hard earning…" is missing a comma.
- "The rain being so hard" is literary, and the collocation should be *heavy* (cf. his own "big rain → heavy rain").
- a/an should be explained by sound, not spelling.

**Sentence adverbs**
- "ne aloittavat virkkeen ja ne erotetaan pilkulla" is too absolute (mid-position *probably*, *clearly*, etc.).
- Several items are not sentence adverbs (Explicitly, Potentially, Primarily, Eventually).
- Gloss errors: Admittedly = eittämättä; Predictably = ennustusten mukaan.

**Agreement**
- "Social media have changed our lives" is marked wrong, but both numbers are accepted, and Maxx's own page uses plural *media*.
- virheiden-rooli says "he has / she have" (typo: *he has* is correct).

**Spelling list**
- "Lapp" (pejorative), "the Samis" (→ the Sámi), "Swedish speaking" (→ hyphen), "loppu = finish" (→ end).

**Idioms, style and register (AI pages)**
- "the last straw that broke the camel's back" conflates two expressions.
- "Still today" is Finnish-influenced; "From a broader view" is unidiomatic.
- "Some see…, others see…" is a comma splice.
- The conditional mismatch "is great, but only if bills didn't exist".
- Many garbled meme examples.
- Meme templates are unsuitable for a formal YO essay; "because + noun" is nonstandard in writing.

**Minor slips in Maxx's own text**
- "privacy on their free time" (→ in), "non-sequencial" (→ non-sequential), "he world's", "säännötkin usein votavat".
